1.0.33

New
- Fitness is now a fourth bar beside hygiene, stress and home, with its last two weeks and what moved it on the Health app's You page. What you eat and drink now moves fitness a little, so meals at home hold it up and fast food and heavy nights wear it down. The police and the physical trades ask for a minimum fitness, and a worker far below it gets a warning, never the sack by itself. Ambulances and police now answer a call like an emergency: sent within seconds, driving fast with lights and siren, their crews running to the casualty and the road clear sooner, and every call says roughly when help will arrive. Answer all on Flare's Business page, and in the old Flare app, answers every waiting review of one business or all of yours by the rules for answering, after showing what it will do, in words written for the kind of business. A manager can take the new duty Answers reviews to do it each morning and report each Sunday, and the option Managers answer reviews switches that off. Tech firms now get work from city departments, some as public tenders that rival firms bid on, from individuals and from the city's own companies, each with a brief in the client's own words and its own pay, requirements and risks. Your tech firms can now change roles together from the Team tab, a game studio's page says which roles build design, art, tech, content and polish and what the team lacks, and a person can be named a firm's manager who balances roles, re-hires, agrees routine raises and flags burnout. The Tech app's team lines now say how many people of each role reach half and nine tenths of the most they can give, how many more that is, and what one more person adds. The way up now explains each of its seven steps and where to do them, lists what is met and what is not with the exact figures, and says what a finished step gave you, on both interfaces. A new option, Game development pace, builds games a third faster by default (Brisk) or twice as fast (Fast), and the days it saves are paid for as tools, contractors and overtime so a game costs the same. You can see a second person in secret and be found out, nights out can follow drinks or a party, and a night can bring a child. Scandals reach the City Ledger and Flare. The partner's page now says what comes next, up to marriage. People now have a hidden passion, from a Cinephile to a Child of the Earth, that shapes how they react to you, and you find it out through outings, texts, small moments and gifts. About one outing in three now has a small moment, such as a queue jumper, a busker or the fire alarm in the big kiss scene, where you choose how to react and they remember what you did. People now drop hints about what they love and hate, and when they introduce their friends, the friends quiz you on how well you listened. Somebody you meet now starts with a little more or less trust, by what they have heard of your name. Two sliders under Friends and social life set how much younger and older than you somebody you could fall for may be.

Fixed
- Several days passing at once, such as a long time skip, no longer cost fitness for days still inside the grace after your last exercise.
- Data centres no longer take software, web or graphic design firms as colocation clients, and offers from such businesses are withdrawn.
- The pause menu named the next day of the week, a work card called today by tomorrow's name, and a place's opening hours on the map stood under the wrong days with Sunday twice.
- The Career app now knows where each employer's help-wanted board is as soon as a game loads, even indoors, and says plainly when a board is not up yet instead of claiming the city has none.
- The Professions detail keeps the employer selector in sight above Show on map and scrolls when it is long, and a long employer name no longer runs under the selector's arrows.
- The All apps list no longer scrolls a page past its last app, a supplier contract's call card names the district in words, the recruitment confirm line says 1 day instead of 1 days, and a candidate's asking wage in the lists matches the figure the negotiation asks.
- A reply from Answer all or from a manager greets a resident by their first name instead of their Flare handle, and a gift shop, florist or hairdresser answers in a shop's words instead of a clothes shop's.
- An emoji the game cannot draw no longer shows as a question mark in a written text, and a manager's month says 1 decision instead of 1 decisions.
- Contacts opened on someone from another app, such as a candidate's Negotiate, no longer keeps an old search that hid them from the list.
- A person no longer lives in Global on their People page or in texts and news about them. They live in a real district.
