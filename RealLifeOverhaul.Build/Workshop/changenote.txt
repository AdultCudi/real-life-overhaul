0.2.1: The city now has an economy of its own. A central bank sets a rate each month, and bank offers, overdrafts and cards follow it. Prices rise or fall, and rents, wages, wholesale prices and takings follow them. There are booms, slowdowns and recessions that change how many customers come and what they spend. Each district has an income level that sets what its customers will pay. Wholesale prices move with the season, the weather and what is on in the city, and the paper announces a shortage or a glut the day before. Finance has a page for all of it, The city. Investors: a tycoon may offer money for a business that does well, and you can pitch to angel investors, tycoons and venture firms yourself. They ask questions, check your figures and take a share of the profit each day. There is a stock market of the city's companies, moved by the day's news. Both are in a new Ventures app, with what your buildings earn. Running a business has an app of its own, Company. Staff have a going rate by job and district: they ask for raises, get offers from rivals and may strike. Each business can be insured against fire, theft and an inspector's fine. Campaigns have a cost, an end and a report of what they brought. A return is filed each quarter, an accountant can file it and find savings, and cash kept off the books saves tax until an audit finds it. Businesses need licences that cost money and run out. The Planner lists what is due.
