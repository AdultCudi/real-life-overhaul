New
- Professionals: your programmers, designers, lawyers and HR staff now have a role (36 of them, from back end engineer to accountant), a level from junior to principal, two skills of their own beside the game's, experience, a school and certificates. They learn on the job and from seniors, are reviewed twice a year, ask for rises, can be promoted, sent on courses and exams, and have notice periods, probation, severance and restraint clauses. Rivals come for your seniors. See the new Open Roles app. A jobs board in Open Roles: post ads for any professional role, screen applicants from one labour market the whole city shares (rivals post and hire from it too), check references and records, interview, make offers and match counter-offers, or brief a search firm for senior people. The recruitment office now brings more candidates than you ask for, matched to the role your business needs. The tech sector: start a Software Studio, Game Studio, IT Consultancy, Cybersecurity Firm or AI Lab from the game's own list in an office building, build subscription services, apps, licensed software and games feature by feature against a rival listed on the stock market, win customers, take on client contracts and bid for the council's tenders, and deal with outages, data breaches, fines and technical debt, all in the new Tech app. Your web agencies and graphic designers take contracts with their spare hours, and research and development relief comes back each quarter. Startups and venture capital: the city's startups grow, raise rounds led by fictional venture firms, take down rounds, fail or are bought, and come to you for money. Meet the founders, buy due diligence, back them as an angel or from a fund of your own, haggle and follow on. Your own tech firms can raise too: pitch to the venture firms, take a term sheet, answer to a board with growth targets that can replace a chief executive, take a bridge loan and grant share options. Data centres, the cloud and AI: fit out a warehouse as a data centre, buy racks of servers and AI accelerators, pay their power and cooling water at the utilities' tariff, staff them and keep them up; let racks to companies, then launch a cloud of your own that sells compute to the city and to your own firms, fights the rival clouds' price war and grows into a hyperscaler with regions across the city. AI labs train models on that compute, or on compute rented from a rival.
