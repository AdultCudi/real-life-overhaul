# Startup exits

## In a sentence

A funded or bootstrapped tech firm of the player's can be bought (a listed company or a startup of the city makes an offer, priced by revenue and growth), can go to the market through bankers, a roadshow, a pricing day and a 180 day lock-up, and then trades as a stock of its own in the Stocks app; before that it can sell some of its owner's shares at a later round. A failing firm may be taken for its team. The city's startups are real places: a founder who is a person of the city, a floor of a tech tower, people hired from the market the player hires from. Built on [Startup money](startup-money.md): every sale pays through the firm account.

- The game's side: `Scripts/Tech/TechDirector.Exits.cs` (the day, the sale, the listing), `TechDirector.ExitStock.cs` (the stock's day, making it, the founder's sales, its end, the test), `TechDirector.ExitsTests.cs`, `Scripts/Wiring/Wiring.ExitLinks.cs` (the market's banks and listed companies, the game's hand over of a business, the population's record, two desk sessions), `Wiring.ExitStockLinks.cs` (the bridges to the market, the firms' statements for the tape), `Scripts/Ui/Tech/TechLive.Exits.cs` (the pages).
- Option: none of its own; it runs with Startups and does nothing without them.

## Offers

A firm taking `RoundBar[Seed]` a month or more draws an offer on the day, seeded: 15 per mille a day, plus one for each 25 basis points of its month's growth (the firms table's own change), scaled by the market's mood, at most two standing, ten days each. The price is `FirmsRules.Value` (a multiple of revenue that grows with growth) scaled by the mood, plus a premium: 10 to 30 per cent from a listed company, up to 15 from a startup that has the cash. Accept, decline, or counter once (a tenth, a fifth or a third more): the chance rises with Selling, Analysis and Relationships. The same choices are a desk session, "An offer for your firm". Offers are never backdated.

## The sale

The business passes to the buyer by the game's own hand over of a business (`ServicesDirector.HandOver`: staff go along, the lease ends); the buyer's money comes into the wallet and the investors' lines come out of it, before the player has anything. `ExitRules.Settle`: the pot is the price, what the firm account holds and a director's loan still out, less the bridge (paid first); `VentureRules.Waterfall` shares it as it stands; the venture firms and the public are owed their lines; the rest is the player's. What the price and wallet cannot cover is the investors' claim (and the charge for a clear pattern), as at a wind down. A sale for nothing is exactly a wind down. Money counted as taken is inside the pot the investors share, so it is paid from the player's proceeds first or stays owed.

Staff option holders get no cash.

## A sale agreed inside the firm

A business cannot change hands with its owner in it, and a desk session sits at a desk inside the firm. So accepting at the desk (`TechDirector.ExitSession`, choice 0) while the game's own refusal is "inside" keeps the player's word instead of refusing: `ExitRules.Agree` records the offer, its price as agreed, its last day, the day and the buyer on the firm's `FirmExit.Agreed` (save line `reallife:tech_exits`, part `a`, which an older reader ignores and an older save lacks), and says the sale completes when they step outside. Any other refusal (a vehicle or gift kept in the building, the game busy) is named at the desk as before.

The sale completes through `ExitAccept`, the one path every accepted offer takes, with no new way of paying out (`Scripts/Tech/TechDirector.ExitAgreed.cs`):

- when the game's exit building event fires (`GlobalEvents.onExitBuilding`, kept on the event by the one-second timer as the other features keep theirs) a flag is set, and the timer looks at the agreed sales once, a second later, when the game's fade is done;
- at the first look after a load, whether the save was made inside the firm or the load is outside;
- on the day's pass (`AgreedDay`), which also lapses a sale whose offer's last day has passed, with a notice, and forgets one whose offer went another way or whose firm is no longer the player's.

The refusals are the same ones, by the same texts (`ExitBridges.WhyNotHand`): one is named once for each reason and firm, not at every exit; the game being busy is asked again the next second; being inside the firm again says nothing and waits for the next exit. The player can call a sale off from the firm's Exits page on both interfaces ("Call off the sale"); the offer then stands to its last day as before. While a sale is agreed the page shows "Agreed: ..." in place of Accept and Decline for that offer and offers no counter to it; turning the offer down by other means (the desk session's "turn it down") ends the agreement. Nothing runs a frame: while no sale is agreed the timer's cost is one comparison. Check `EconomyCheck --exit-agreed` (part of `--startup-exits`).

## Capital gains

Every way the player is paid for a stake notes a capital gain on the day: a firm sold (and a team taken for the hire), the vested shares sold after a round or after a lock-up, and an angel stake sold with a startup of the city. The gain is what the wallet gained less the cost basis, never under nothing, and joins the quarter's return beside a business sale's gain (`CompanyBridges.StartupGains`, summed with `OtherIncome` by `TaxRules.OtherIncome`). The basis is what the player paid for shares (`Holder.Invested`) and what shares bought back cost (`BasisLine.Bought`, the price with the commission, kept at the start's prices), what is left of both spread over the shares held and used in proportion to the shares sold, and, for a firm sold with its business, the stock and furniture at the game's resale price in the player's share of the firm. The incorporation fee and running costs were deductible when paid and are not counted again. A founder's deal in the Stocks app is noted the same way, once for each deal (`ExitGains.ForMarketDeal`, called from `TechDirector.FirmStockDealt` before the cap table is aligned): a sale notes what the wallet gained (the market's money, whole dollars made cents, less the commission and any short swing paid back, already in today's prices) less the basis at today's prices, joining the quarter's return like `ExitSell`'s; a purchase notes no gain and adds what it cost, commission included, to the stake's basis, so a later sale of shares bought back, on the Exits page, on the market or to a buyer's offer, is taxed on what it fetched less what they cost. The player's whole company on the market has no cap table but the same rules: its founder's deals, a buy-back and going private (money put into the stake without shares) and a bid that wins, from a private buyer, a rival tycoon or the test button, tell the tax through `MarketBridges.CompanyDealt` and `CompanyTaken` (`TechDirector.CompanyDealt`, `CompanyTaken`, basis line `#company`); a takeover takes the founder's part of the handed over businesses' stock and furniture off the gain, as a firm sold with its business does. The file is read from the save when the venture part is off. A save from before has no cost of shares bought back, so those shares carry none, as they did; nothing is charged for the past. City startups and rivals have no return. Checks `--exit-market-gain` and `--exit-basis`. Save line `reallife:tech_exit_gains`; check `--exit-tax`.

## The way to the market

"Ask for a listing" opens the firm's page of exits. Bankers (the stock market's three largest banks, the largest reaches furthest and keeps most), a retainer paid by the firm; a roadshow (desk session or page: wide, deep, quick) sets the demand from the market's mood, Selling, Relationships and the bank's reach and fixes the range and the pricing day; the pricing day draws the price inside the range (called off under 85 per cent of fair value); the public buys a fifth, the money after the bank's spread reaches the firm as money raised (the wallet must keep it), and the player's shares are locked up 180 days. The firm then becomes a stock (below); its public line stays in its cap table (`HolderKind.Public`). The whole company's own listing stays one button away. A firm whose investors' money is counted as taken, or with the board's question, demand or removal open, is not taken to the market: the bankers are not engaged and a book at its pricing day is called off, because the public is not sold shares of a firm that owes.

## Shares sold later

After a priced round, for 30 days, the player may sell a fifth of their vested shares to the round's investors at the round's price less 10 to 25 per cent (Selling and Relationships take it down). The money is the player's own, booked with no address; the firm account and the floor are not touched. Once a firm's shares trade, none of this applies to it: its shares are sold in the Stocks app only (a refusal says so, and a click on it opens the stock), and no private round is closed.

## A firm that listed is a stock

When the pricing day lists a firm, the offer's money goes into the firm account as before (`VentureGame.Give` and `StartupMoney.Inflow`, once) and the tech sector then asks the market for the firm's stock (`TechDirector.StockAfterListing`, `StockBridges.Make`). Nothing is paid for it and no money moves. It has the firm's name, a ticker of three or four capitals no company has, the sector of the firm's trade, the offer's price (the firm's value before the money over the shares before it) and all the shares in issue as the cap table has them.

It is the market's existing player company (`OwnListing`), marked `Firm`, with its firm's place, and not a second model. Its founder's stake is `OwnListing.Founder`, the public's shares `Float`, the 180 day lock-up `LockDay` (the tech sector's `ExitLockLeft` reads this one) and the founder's vesting the cap table's own schedule (`VestBase`, `VestDay`, `VestCliff`, `VestSpan`, `VentureRules.Vested`). The Stocks screen draws it with the player company rows. It differs from the player's whole company in these ways, each so that no money comes out twice: it keeps no reserve and declares no dividend (the holders' share of its profit stays in the firm account); only the founder's deals are open, so pay, dividends, buy-backs, new shares and going private are refused (`OwnWhy.Firm`); the founder may sell only shares that have vested (`OwnWhy.Unvested`) and only after the lock-up (`OwnWhy.FirmLocked`), and buys only what the public holds; no takeover bid is made for it and it never splits (a firm is bought through the tech sector's offers); bots never trade it.

One source of truth: once the stock trades, it is the one that moves. `ExitStockRules.Align` makes the cap table's player and public lines what the stock says each day and before a sale, a wind down or a removal; shares made since (staff options, a co-founder) flow into the stock (`Grown`, `MarketEngine.GrowFirm`); a founder removed as a bad leaver loses the unvested shares in the stock as in the cap table (`MarketEngine.CancelUnvested`). The founder's sale on the market is told to the tech sector as it is made (`MarketBridges.FirmDealt`, `TechDirector.FirmStockDealt`); that is where a gain on the shares sold is noted, in place of `ExitSell`.

Its days are the firm's own: each market morning the statements at the firm's address for the days since the last open ride on the tape after the player company's eight columns (`MarketTape.WriteEnv`, so an older reader reads the day it always did). The takings of a firm that listed are kept out of the whole company's books, places and trades. Quarterly results, the price, charts and the founder's closed periods are the market's own.

It ends with the firm. A sale (an offer accepted) takes the stock off the market as taken over; a wind down as delisted, or failed when the firm honestly ran dry; each share is paid what the cap table's waterfall pays the public's line over the public's shares, out of the pot the waterfall shared, so the investors are never paid twice. A firm gone from the tech sector otherwise (set aside while its player was a guest, wound down meanwhile) is swept off the market paying nothing. Offers for such a firm are priced off all its shares at the market's last price.

The city's startups that list are companies of the market's own (`MarketEngine.ListStartup`, ids 2001 to 2099, at most six live): the listing's name, a ticker, the sector of what they do, their price and shares, run by the market's model of a company in the sector. Seeded by the day they list, on the host's or a lone player's market only.

A firm that had already listed in an older save is given its stock on the first day it is seen, at its worth by its books over all its shares, with its own listing day and its own lock-up: no fee, no money, no first morning's jump.

Going Public: the host's market part carries the firm's stock as it does the player company; the statements ride on the tape, so every machine plays the same days; a guest's own firms are set aside in the market line's `~v:` part and come back when they play alone again; nothing new is sent that an older or absent RLO would have to read.

Save: `reallife:tech_exit_stock` (the tech sector's record of each firm's stock and how it ended; the venture and exits lines are untouched, and an old save loads with none). The market's line has the firm's fields after the figures an older version reads, and the firm's place after the mix.

This is the only way the player sells their own shares. The firm page's "Sell my shares" button opens this page, and the sale keeps its rules: vested shares only, inside the window, the round's limit counted in the save line `reallife:tech_exits` (so a reload gives no room back), the lock-up after a listing, and the capital gain noted once. The older button that sold a tenth of all shares at four fifths of the post-money value is gone. A player who sold unvested shares with it keeps what they got and has nothing more to sell until shares vest. City startups and rivals never sell through this path.

## Acqui-hire

A funded firm with under 45 days of its account left, burning, may be taken for its team at 300 thousandths of what was put in. Same settlement: investors get what the waterfall gives, and no claim follows when nothing was taken. The player is offered a job, once for each sale: `TechDirector.ExitJobFor` (after the business is handed over) asks Careers for a lead through `ExitBridges.Job` (`CareersDirector.ExitLead`, an ordinary opening and application, nothing started and no job, firm or money of the player touched). A startup of the city that has a floor of a tower and a tech post the player is qualified for gives a post at its own office; a listed company, or no such post, gives a real vacancy of the city in a profession the player is qualified for, and the notice says the buyer's people put the name forward. Somebody with a job is offered only a better post. With no vacancy the player is told so once. A second acqui-hire while the first lead is open gives no lead. The sale is entered in `reallife:tech_exit_jobs` (`ExitJobRules.cs`, `ExitJobStore.cs`) before the lead is asked for, so a reload or a repeat cannot give two; an older save has no line and no lead. A city startup bought for its team lets its founder go to the buyer: `PopulationLinks.Employ` moves their employer record (the buyer's office building if it has a vacancy, else a vacancy of the city's in their field). Records only. Checks: `EconomyCheck --exit-job`; options tests "A buyer puts me forward for a job now" and "A city startup's founder is taken on by a buyer now".

## The city's startups

Each startup has a founder record (age, sex, skill): a resident linked from the living population (`LinkKind.Founder`, from the field its sector works in) when it runs, an invented one when not. It lets a place in one of the towers (fewest startups first, seeded), shown above the companies' floors (`Headquarters.PlaceStartups`), with its address and a Set destination button on its page. It takes people from the labour market the player hires from (`ExitPlaces.Hire`), marking them taken, so a hire one makes is gone for the other. Startups already in an older save get all this on the first day seen.

## Terms of a sale

On the exits page the player picks how an offer is paid before the game's confirm box, and every figure is on the page and again in the box (`SaleTermText`). An acqui-hire is cash only. Rules, game-free: `Scripts/Tech/Venture/SaleTermRules.cs` (a plan from the offer, the deal, its monthly steps), `SaleTermStore.cs` (save line `reallife:tech_sale_terms`, apart from the exits' line; a save from before has none), `SaleTermText.cs` (the words, shared by both interfaces); the game's side `TechDirector.SaleTerms.cs`, `TechDirector.Exits.cs` (`ExitAccept` takes the choice), the pages `Ui/Tech/TechLive.Exits.cs`, `TechLive.Deals.cs` and `Tech/Venture/TechApp.Exits.cs`, `TechApp.Venture.cs`. The deals open are listed with the startups held, each with a page. The investors' waterfall is worked out on the whole price as before; what is held back or kept is a part of the price, never more than the player's own part after the investors, so the investors are paid in full at the sale and the wallet never pays for a deferral. The capital gain is noted on the whole of the player's part at the sale, as before; an earn-out's prize over what was held back, and a stake's dividends and any buyout over its price, are noted when they arrive.

- **Part in the buyer's shares.** Only when the buyer is a listed company of the market (an offer already names its buyer by stock id, `ExitOffer.BuyerId`) and the market can be asked (`MarketDirector.FirmsOpen`: not a guest). A quarter, half or three quarters of the price is paid in whole shares at the day's price; what is under one share stays cash. The shares go in the portfolio at what they were worth (`MarketEngine.Grant`), with a lock-up of 180 days kept in `MarketState.Locks` (the desk's part of the market's line, so an older reader never sees it). A sale of locked shares is refused at the order, at the fill and for a worked order (`OrderWhy.Locked`); the portfolio of both interfaces shows how many are locked and until which day.
- **Keep a slice.** A tenth, a fifth or three tenths of the firm is kept for that much less cash now. The firm is no longer the player's, so its takings move by one seeded draw a month (`SaleTermRules.Drift`, from the save's seed, the deal and the month: the same on every machine) from the figure at the sale. The stake pays its share of a profit of a quarter of the takings each month, and at its end, three years on, the buyer buys it at its share of the price moved with the firm, less a minority discount. It never pays more than three times what it cost.
- **Earn-out.** Three tenths of the price is held back. The written target is a tenth, a quarter or 35 per cent more takings (or customers) than at the sale, a year on; the prize is 1.1, 1.8 or 3 times the held back sum. Judged on the due day, paid or lapsed with one notice. Progress is the firm's modelled figure.
- **Stay as the boss.** The sale does not hand the business over: it stays the player's in the game, in the Tech app, with its staff, products and desk sessions, and the contract is the player's as its boss for three or four years. Cash now is the price less a grant (a quarter, vesting a year at a time: in the buyer's shares at the day's price when it is listed, else a cash bonus) and an earn-out (15 per cent, judged on the firm's real takings or customers a year on, with the same written targets). A salary is paid each 30 days (8 per cent of the month's takings at the sale, inside the founders' salary band of the firm account). The firm is the buyer's: each month its profit is paid out of the wallet to the buyer (`TermRemitKey`) and the buyer carries a loss up to 30 per cent of the sale's month of takings (`TermCoverKey`), so the firm's money is not the player's. Every 90 days the buyer reads the takings: under 70 per cent of the sale's month is a strike, a good reading clears it, and two strikes in a row dismiss the boss. Resigning or dismissal lose the years not vested and an earn-out not judged. At the end of the term, a resignation or a dismissal the business is handed over to the buyer by the game's own sale of a business, tried each day until the game allows it (not while the player is inside, a vehicle or gift is kept in it, or in a Going Public session); until then the buyer still has the profit.

  *Whose the firm is, and when.* From the day of the sale the firm is the buyer's in every figure: the cap table the sale ended shows the buyer as the whole owner (`TechDirector.OwnShare` reads 0 for the player; both interfaces' cap pages and funding rows), and the firm is held in every contract state, running, resigned, dismissed or at its term, until the business has actually left the player (`TermDeal.Live`: open, or a boss deal not yet `Handed`; `TechDirector.Contracted`, read from the save when the venture part is off). A held firm takes no offer (`FirmExitsDay`), has no desk session (`ExitBlocked`), cannot be sold a second time (`ExitAccept` and `ExitAgree` refuse and drop an offer left standing), raises no round, grants no options, names no chief, and is offered no way to the market on either interface (the bankers, the roadshow and the whole company's listing are refused with a reason, `ContractRefused`, and the exits page says why instead of offering them); the whole company's listing, hand-over and trade leave it out (`ListingLinks.Apart`); the mod's own sale of businesses refuses it (`reallife_svc_refuse_contract`); a takeover of the player's listed company leaves it be.

  *The hand-over.* `TermHand` runs on each day for an ended contract that is not handed over. It first settles the days up to today with the buyer (`TermRemit`), then calls `ServicesDirector.HandOver(place, buyer, byContract: true)`: the contract's own hand-over is not stopped by the hold the contract puts on the business (1.0.19 let that hold answer "not yours any more", so the contract was marked handed over and the firm stayed the player's, with the cap table gone, its offers back and a second sale possible). A hand-over that says it is done is believed only when `ExitBridges.StillHeld` says the business is gone. What stands in the way (inside, a vehicle, a gift, stock, the game busy, the sales off or a guest) is named by one notice when it first waits, when the cause changes and once a week (`TellHandWait`, `SaleTermRules.WaitKey`), and it is tried again every day. A contract is not made where it could not be ended (the sales of businesses off, or a Going Public session).

  *The gap's money.* While the contract runs the buyer carries a loss up to 30 per cent of the sale's month of takings; the days after the contract ended (`SaleTermRules.GapDays`, from `EndDay`, which a resignation now dates to the day it was made) are the buyer's whole, loss included (`SaleTermRules.Cover`). The profit is the buyer's all along.

  *A fee for a listing that cannot happen.* The way to the market is refused with the reason before any retainer is taken. A way begun for a firm that a contract came to hold, or one that fails on the pricing day, ends with the retainer given back on the retainer's own bank line and one notice (`CancelWay`, `GiveFeeBack`); the book called off because the firm owes its investors gives the retainer back too, while one called off for the market's price keeps it as before. 1.0.19 took the fee and then `ListFirm` ended silently as the firm had no cap table.

  *A save from 1.0.19.* At the first load `MendContracts` runs once (the mark `TermFile.Mended` is saved in the header of the deals' line): a contract marked handed over though the business is still the player's, of the same firm that was sold (`Firm.Founded` not after the sale), is opened again so the hand-over is made, with one notice; retainers paid for a firm a contract held (a way on the road, or priced after the sale and ended with nothing) are given back once, with one notice. Nothing the player was paid is taken back. A firm that was sold a second time is gone and needs nothing.

  *The chief executive.* The selector on the cap page sets `Cap.Ceo`. Only the board reads it: when investors hold the board and the firm keeps missing its target, `ReplaceChief` lets a professional named chief go and brings in a new one, or puts a new one in the player's chair (`Remove` replaces the chief on a founder's removal only when the player is chief). Nothing else reads it, so the page says so.

Constants (`SaleTermCatalogue`; cents at the start's prices, a month 30 days and a year 360 as the exits count their lock-up and quarters), each with its reason: share steps 250, 500, 750 (the player's own quarter and half, and a buyer's stock is not trusted with more than three quarters); lock-up 180 days (the lock-up a firm's own listing gives its founder); slice steps 100, 200, 300 (always a minority, never over a third); slice term 3 years and contract years 3 or 4 (the player's own "three to four years"); minority discount 200 (a stake that cannot decide anything is worth less); slice margin 250 (a software firm's profit, between games and consultancies); slice cap 300 per cent (the stake can never be worth more than three times its price, whatever the draws); earn-out share 300 and boss earn-out 150 (a held-back part large enough to matter, small enough to leave most of the price); targets 1100, 1250, 1350 and prizes 1100, 1800, 3000 (with the drift below a firm reaches a tenth more in about 86 runs of a hundred, a quarter more in 51 and a third more in 27, so each prize is worth 95, 92 and 81 per cent of the held back sum on average: the likelier the target the smaller the prize); earn-out due 360 days (a year); drift -40 to +80 a month (a year's growth averages a quarter more with room either side) and an index kept between 300 and 3000 (no firm doubles or dies in a season); boss grant 250 (a quarter of the price, vesting yearly); salary 800 basis points of the month's takings inside the firm account's salary band; review every 90 days (a quarter, as the board's pack is monthly and a verdict needs more than one month); keep 700 and two strikes (a slump of a third is no accident; one bad quarter is forgiven); loss cover 300 (a buyer's patience, so a boss cannot spend the buyer's money without limit); deals kept 24 and open at once 6 (the daily pass stays a handful of lines).

Tax. Each payment is one thing to the tax, once (`SaleTermRules.Taxing`): a stake's dividend is a dividend, taxed as the stock market's dividends are (`ListingVotes.DividendTax`, the council's business tax moving the rate; `ExitBridges.DividendTax`, a bank line `tech_terms_dividend_tax`); a stake's buyout and an earn-out's prize are capital gains over what was held back (the sale noted its whole part at the time), noted by `ExitGains.Note`; a salary, a vested bonus and vested shares are pay for work, which the game and the mod tax at nothing (the pay slip's tax line reads zero), so they are in no tax line. A contract firm's profit is the owner's: the remit is booked as a deduction the game's own tax reads (`VentureGame.PayDeductible`, `SetTaxDeductibleName`), and the mod's own return takes it out of the player's takings by an adjustment line (`SaleTermRules.Hold` writes a line for the days the profit covers, `Held` reads them in proportion, `CompanyBridges.HeldProfit` into `CompanyGame.Other`, the one sum the return, its page and the reserve read), so nothing is taxed twice and nothing escapes. A loss the owner carried puts the takings back by as much. The ledger is saved in part `t` of the deals' line.

The desk. The offer's session keeps accept, counter and decline as its first three choices and adds four after them: half in shares, a fifth kept, an earn-out at the middle target, a three year contract (`TechDirector.SessionTerms` gives each as the `TermChoice` the page builds). A sale agreed at the desk and completed later must call `TechDirector.ExitAccept(place, offerId, choice)` with the `TermChoice` {Kind, Step, Target, Measure} stored when it was agreed; the plan is worked out again at completion from the offer's price and the firm's figures then.

Options, Tests: "Move my sale terms a year on" shifts every deal a year back and runs the day. Check `EconomyCheck --sale-terms`.
