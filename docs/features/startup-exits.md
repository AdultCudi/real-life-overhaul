# Startup exits

## In a sentence

A funded or bootstrapped tech firm of the player's can be bought (a listed company or a startup of the city makes an offer, priced by revenue and growth), can go to the market through bankers, a roadshow, a pricing day and a 180 day lock-up, and then trades as a stock of its own in the Stocks app; before that it can sell some of its owner's shares at a later round. A failing firm may be taken for its team. The city's startups are real places: a founder who is a person of the city, a floor of a tech tower, people hired from the market the player hires from. Built on [Startup money](startup-money.md): every sale pays through the firm account.

- Option: none of its own; it runs with Startups and does nothing without them.

## Offers

A firm taking `RoundBar[Seed]` a month or more draws an offer on the day, seeded: 15 per mille a day, plus one for each 25 basis points of its month's growth (the firms table's own change), scaled by the market's mood, at most two standing, ten days each. The price is `FirmsRules.Value` (a multiple of revenue that grows with growth) scaled by the mood, plus a premium: 10 to 30 per cent from a listed company, up to 15 from a startup that has the cash. Accept, decline, or counter once (a tenth, a fifth or a third more): the chance rises with Selling, Analysis and Relationships. The same choices are a desk session, "An offer for your firm". Offers are never backdated.

## The sale

The business passes to the buyer by the game's own hand over of a business (`ServicesDirector.HandOver`: staff go along, the lease ends); the buyer's money comes into the wallet and the investors' lines come out of it, before the player has anything. `ExitRules.Settle`: the pot is the price, what the firm account holds and a director's loan still out, less the bridge (paid first); `VentureRules.Waterfall` shares it as it stands; the venture firms and the public are owed their lines; the rest is the player's. What the price and wallet cannot cover is the investors' claim (and the charge for a clear pattern), as at a wind down. A sale for nothing is exactly a wind down. Money counted as taken is inside the pot the investors share, so it is paid from the player's proceeds first or stays owed.

Staff option holders get no cash.

## Capital gains

Every way the player is paid for a stake notes a capital gain on the day: a firm sold (and a team taken for the hire), the vested shares sold after a round or after a lock-up, and an angel stake sold with a startup of the city. The gain is what the wallet gained less the cost basis, never under nothing, and joins the quarter's return beside a business sale's gain (`CompanyBridges.StartupGains`, summed with `OtherIncome` by `TaxRules.OtherIncome`). The basis is what the player paid for shares (`Holder.Invested`) and what shares bought back cost (`BasisLine.Bought`, the price with the commission, kept at the start's prices), what is left of both spread over the shares held and used in proportion to the shares sold, and, for a firm sold with its business, the stock and furniture at the game's resale price in the player's share of the firm. The incorporation fee and running costs were deductible when paid and are not counted again. A founder's deal in the Stocks app is noted the same way, once for each deal (`ExitGains.ForMarketDeal`, called from `TechDirector.FirmStockDealt` before the cap table is aligned): a sale notes what the wallet gained (the market's money, whole dollars made cents, less the commission and any short swing paid back, already in today's prices) less the basis at today's prices, joining the quarter's return like `ExitSell`'s; a purchase notes no gain and adds what it cost, commission included, to the stake's basis, so a later sale of shares bought back, on the Exits page, on the market or to a buyer's offer, is taxed on what it fetched less what they cost. The player's whole company on the market has no cap table but the same rules: its founder's deals, a buy-back and going private (money put into the stake without shares) and a bid that wins, from a private buyer, a rival tycoon or the test button, tell the tax through `MarketBridges.CompanyDealt` and `CompanyTaken` (`TechDirector.CompanyDealt`, `CompanyTaken`, basis line `#company`); a takeover takes the founder's part of the handed over businesses' stock and furniture off the gain, as a firm sold with its business does. The file is read from the save when the venture part is off. A save from before has no cost of shares bought back, so those shares carry none, as they did; nothing is charged for the past. City startups and rivals have no return. Checks `--exit-market-gain` and `--exit-basis`. Save line `reallife:tech_exit_gains`; check `--exit-tax`.

## The way to the market

"Ask for a listing" opens the firm's page of exits. Bankers (the stock market's three largest banks, the largest reaches furthest and keeps most), a retainer paid by the firm; a roadshow (desk session or page: wide, deep, quick) sets the demand from the market's mood, Selling, Relationships and the bank's reach and fixes the range and the pricing day; the pricing day draws the price inside the range (called off under 85 per cent of fair value); the public buys a fifth, the money after the bank's spread reaches the firm as money raised (the wallet must keep it), and the player's shares are locked up 180 days. The firm then becomes a stock (below); its public line stays in its cap table (`HolderKind.Public`). The whole company's own listing stays one button away. A firm whose investors' money is counted as taken, or with the board's question, demand or removal open, is not taken to the market: the bankers are not engaged and a book at its pricing day is called off, because the public is not sold shares of a firm that owes.

## Shares sold later

After a priced round, for 30 days, the player may sell a fifth of their vested shares to the round's investors at the round's price less 10 to 25 per cent (Selling and Relationships take it down). The money is the player's own, booked with no address; the firm account and the floor are not touched. Once a firm's shares trade, none of this applies to it: its shares are sold in the Stocks app only (a refusal says so, and a click on it opens the stock), and no private round is closed.

## A firm that listed is a stock

When the pricing day lists a firm, the offer's money goes into the firm account as before (`VentureGame.Give` and `StartupMoney.Inflow`, once) and the tech sector then asks the market for the firm's stock (`TechDirector.StockAfterListing`, `StockBridges.Make`). Nothing is paid for it and no money moves. It has the firm's name, a ticker of three or four capitals no company has, the sector of the firm's trade, the offer's price (the firm's value before the money over the shares before it) and all the shares in issue as the cap table has them.

It is the market's existing player company (`OwnListing`), marked `Firm`, with its firm's place, and not a second model. Its founder's stake is `OwnListing.Founder`, the public's shares `Float`, the 180 day lock-up `LockDay` (the tech sector's `ExitLockLeft` reads this one) and the founder's vesting the cap table's own schedule (`VestBase`, `VestDay`, `VestCliff`, `VestSpan`, `VentureRules.Vested`). The Stocks screen draws it with the player company rows. It differs from the player's whole company in these ways, each so that no money comes out twice: it keeps no reserve and declares no dividend (the holders' share of its profit stays in the firm account); only the founder's deals are open, so pay, dividends, buy-backs, new shares and going private are refused (`OwnWhy.Firm`); the founder may sell only shares that have vested (`OwnWhy.Unvested`) and only after the lock-up (`OwnWhy.FirmLocked`), and buys only what the public holds; no takeover bid is made for it and it never splits (a firm is bought through the tech sector's offers); bots never trade it.

One source of truth: once the stock trades, it is the one that moves. `ExitStockRules.Align` makes the cap table's player and public lines what the stock says each day and before a sale, a wind down or a removal; shares made since (staff options, a co-founder) flow into the stock (`Grown`, `MarketEngine.GrowFirm`); a founder removed as a bad leaver loses the unvested shares in the stock as in the cap table (`MarketEngine.CancelUnvested`). The founder's sale on the market is told to the tech sector as it is made (`MarketBridges.FirmDealt`, `TechDirector.FirmStockDealt`); that is where a gain on the shares sold is noted, in place of `ExitSell`.

Its days are the firm's own: each market morning the statements at the firm's address for the days since the last open ride on the tape after the player company's eight columns (`MarketTape.WriteEnv`, so an older reader reads the day it always did). The takings of a firm that listed are kept out of the whole company's books, places and trades. Quarterly results, the price, charts and the founder's closed periods are the market's own.

It ends with the firm. A sale (an offer accepted) takes the stock off the market as taken over; a wind down as delisted, or failed when the firm honestly ran dry; each share is paid what the cap table's waterfall pays the public's line over the public's shares, out of the pot the waterfall shared, so the investors are never paid twice. A firm gone from the tech sector otherwise (set aside while its player was a guest, wound down meanwhile) is swept off the market paying nothing. Offers for such a firm are priced off all its shares at the market's last price.

The city's startups that list are companies of the market's own (`MarketEngine.ListStartup`, ids 2001 to 2099, at most six live): the listing's name, a ticker, the sector of what they do, their price and shares, run by the market's model of a company in the sector. Seeded by the day they list, on the host's or a lone player's market only.

A firm that had already listed in an older save is given its stock on the first day it is seen, at its worth by its books over all its shares, with its own listing day and its own lock-up: no fee, no money, no first morning's jump.

Going Public: the host's market part carries the firm's stock as it does the player company; the statements ride on the tape, so every machine plays the same days; a guest's own firms are set aside in the market line's `~v:` part and come back when they play alone again; nothing new is sent that an older or absent RLO would have to read.

Save: `reallife:tech_exit_stock` (the tech sector's record of each firm's stock and how it ended; the venture and exits lines are untouched, and an old save loads with none). The market's line has the firm's fields after the figures an older version reads, and the firm's place after the mix.

This is the only way the player sells their own shares. The firm page's "Sell my shares" button opens this page, and the sale keeps its rules: vested shares only, inside the window, the round's limit counted in the save line `reallife:tech_exits` (so a reload gives no room back), the lock-up after a listing, and the capital gain noted once. The older button that sold a tenth of all shares at four fifths of the post-money value is gone. A player who sold unvested shares with it keeps what they got and has nothing more to sell until shares vest. City startups and rivals never sell through this path.

## Acqui-hire

A funded firm with under 45 days of its account left, burning, may be taken for its team at 300 thousandths of what was put in. Same settlement: investors get what the waterfall gives, and no claim follows when nothing was taken. The player is offered a job, once for each sale: `TechDirector.ExitJobFor` (after the business is handed over) asks Careers for a lead through `ExitBridges.Job` (`CareersDirector.ExitLead`, an ordinary opening and application, nothing started and no job, firm or money of the player touched). A startup of the city that has a floor of a tower and a tech post the player is qualified for gives a post at its own office; a listed company, or no such post, gives a real vacancy of the city in a profession the player is qualified for, and the notice says the buyer's people put the name forward. Somebody with a job is offered only a better post. With no vacancy the player is told so once. A second acqui-hire while the first lead is open gives no lead. The sale is entered in `reallife:tech_exit_jobs` (`ExitJobRules.cs`, `ExitJobStore.cs`) before the lead is asked for, so a reload or a repeat cannot give two; an older save has no line and no lead. A city startup bought for its team lets its founder go to the buyer: `PopulationLinks.Employ` moves their employer record (the buyer's office building if it has a vacancy, else a vacancy of the city's in their field). Records only. Checks: `EconomyCheck --exit-job`; options tests "A buyer puts me forward for a job now" and "A city startup's founder is taken on by a buyer now".

## The city's startups

Each startup has a founder record (age, sex, skill): a resident linked from the living population (`LinkKind.Founder`, from the field its sector works in) when it runs, an invented one when not. It lets a place in one of the towers (fewest startups first, seeded), shown above the companies' floors (`Headquarters.PlaceStartups`), with its address and a Set destination button on its page. It takes people from the labour market the player hires from (`ExitPlaces.Hire`), marking them taken, so a hire one makes is gone for the other. Startups already in an older save get all this on the first day seen.
