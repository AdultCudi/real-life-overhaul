# The phone: folders, the two apps first, the RLO phone and the Banks app

## In a sentence

The phone's home screen works like a real phone's: the mod's apps stand in five folders, two favourites first on the home screen, the player moves apps with a right click, the More apps button always on a page's last tile, the RLO interface draws the phone as a phone with its page arrows fixed at its foot, and the three bank apps are one Banks app.

## Where the code is

- Folder: [`Scripts/Core/GameUi`](../../Assets/Mods/RealLifeOverhaul/Scripts/Core/GameUi/), beside the pages it extends (`PhoneGridPager`).
- Read first: `PhoneLayoutRules.cs` (game-free: which app stands where, the view of a grid, the tiles of a page), `PhoneLayout.cs` (game-free: the player's moves and their one line), then `PhoneGridPager.cs` (its "FOLDERS AND A DOCK" part).
- The Banks app: [`Scripts/Economy/Banking/BankApps`](../../Assets/Mods/RealLifeOverhaul/Scripts/Economy/Banking/BankApps/); see [the Banks app](economy-banks.md).

## How it talks to other features

- None of the mod's features knows of the folders. An app is put in a folder by its id (its button's name, `PhoneAppDef.Id`) through `PhoneLayoutRules.Defaults`, the same order as `PagerRules.ModOrder`; a feature that adds an app adds its id to both tables (ContractCheck fails an app with no place).
- A badge an app sets on its button (`PhoneApp.SetBadge`) is read back from the button by the pager, whoever set it.

```
Core/GameUi/
  PhoneLayoutRules  game-free: the five folders and the dock, each app's usual place, the view of a grid (the home
                    screen or a folder's page, and the dock row), the tiles a page holds, the empty tiles before the
                    dock, who may move where, a folder's number
  PhoneLayout       game-free: the player's moves over the usual places, written as one line ("1|id=place|...");
                    a part this version cannot read is kept as it was read
  PhoneLayoutPrefs  the layout on this computer (PlayerPrefs "reallife.phone.layout"), and the word that it changed
  PhoneFolders      the tiles of a grid's folders: a copy of the game's app button for each folder with the small
                    pictures of its apps, the Back tile, the empty tiles that keep the dock on the last row
  PhoneFolderKeys   the game's cancel key closes a folder of the HUD phone (on only while one is open);
                    PhoneAppPress: the right click on an app of the mod's that opens the move list
  PhoneMove         the move list (the game's list window, DeskList), the options page's reset and the two tests
  PhoneGridPager    the pages of both phones, now over the view: the home screen's items or a folder's
```

## What the player sees

- **Folders.** The home screen holds the game's own apps first, in the game's order, then a tile for each folder that has an app in it, in this order: Money (Finance, Banks, Your bank, Stocks, Economy, the Newsstand, Utilities), Work and study (Career, Campus, Open Roles), Social and life (the Camera and the Gallery, and the Planner and Flare if moved there), Business (Company, Brands, Services, Ventures, the web shop, Property, the Car Showroom, Tech) and Settings (the Guide and Mod settings). Then the mod's apps the player put on the home screen by themselves, then other mods' buttons as they stand (Going Public's Business, Chat and Hub). The groups are `PagerRules.ModOrder`'s.
- **A folder's tile** is a copy of the game's app button of that phone with the game's scripts that make it an app taken off (as the More apps button is): the folder's name where an app shows its own, a slate tile painted as the game's icons are (`AppIconPainter`) under the small pictures of its first nine apps, three rows of three, and the game's red badge with its apps' numbers added up.
- **A folder's page**: the Back tile first on every page (a light tile with the game's double chevron turned back, the folder's name and "Back" under it; its badge counts the buttons outside the folder with a number up), then the folder's apps in the mod's order, paged as the home screen is (the More apps button on the last tile). Back, the game's cancel key on the HUD phone, or the full menu turning to an app outside the folder go back to the home screen, on the page it was left on.
- **The full menu's top row** has the same folders on its own pager. When the menu opens on an app in a folder, the row shows that folder's page with the app's dot. A row of two tiles (a 4:3 screen) has no room for a Back tile, an app and the More apps button, and keeps the plain pages.
- **Two apps first** (the dock until 2 Nov 2026; the saved place is still "dock"): the HUD phone's home screen leads with them, ahead of the game's own; they start as the Planner and Flare. On the full menu's row they stand first after the game's own. A third (a layout from a time an app was off) stands on the home screen.
- **The More apps button** is a tile of the grid, after the page's apps and the empty tiles that fill a short page, so it stands on the last tile of every page, on the home screen and in every folder, and moves with the grid whatever moves the container (the owner, 1 Nov 2026: "The next button must be at the bottom instead it is random").
- **The RLO phone** (Ui/Phone, with the RLO interface and its Phone switch): the phone drawn as a phone in the kit's look over the game's (UiPhone, PhoneScreen): a body with a thin rim, a status line, the phone's name with its fold-down chevron, the apps on rounded tiles with their names (other mods' with their own pictures), folders as tiles of their first apps' glyphs, what comes next from the Planner and the Calendar, the game's radio row and private driver, and the nav bar with the page arrows and dots fixed at the screen's foot. It stands inside the game's container (it folds and hides with it) at the game's phone's own height, never the container's, which other mods stretch. The game's grid stays alive under it, each of its buttons hidden through the host's UiConceal (never the grid, which other mods fill); every tile presses its button's onClick. The Phone key (F11 at first) or the pad's View gives it the keys; Back leaves a folder, then gives them back. Another pager or another mod's screen on the phone has it stand down to the classic phone for the session.
- **Moving an app**: a right click on an app of the mod's on the classic HUD phone opens the game's list window (the subway's, `DeskList`) with an entry for each folder, the home screen and first on it; the place it is in is marked and cannot be picked, nor first on the home screen while two are there. A pick moves it on both phones at once and a notice says where to.
- **The layout** is kept on this computer with the game's own settings (PlayerPrefs), as the options are: the same phone in every save, and in a Going Public session each player's own. Only moves away from an app's usual place are kept; an app added in a later version starts in its folder.
- **The option** "App folders on the phone" (`phone_folders`, Phone section, on): off, every tile of the folders goes and the pages are the plain ones of before. "Put every app back in its folder" empties the layout.

## The grid's order

The game's grid lays out what shows in the order it stands. The pager puts every child in the view's order whatever the page (`PhoneGridPager.Order`): the Back tile, the view's items (the two first, the game's own apps, folder tiles, loose apps, other mods'), the empty tiles of a short page, the More apps button, then the buttons in closed folders in the mod's order; it moves nothing when that is the order already. A button in a closed folder is switched off with the same `PageMark` as a button on another page, so another mod's pager is still seen at once, and the pager steps aside as before, taking every tile of its own away (`DropFolders`).

## The game's parts

- The game's phone has no folders and no dock; it has the app button (its tile, title, picture and badge, `SmartphoneAppButton`, `FullMenuAppButton`), the grid (`GridLayoutGroup`, two columns, four rows) and the row (`HorizontalLayoutGroup`). The folders are made of those alone.
- The game's badge is `UI.Elements.Badge` (`UpdateBadge`: on above 0, "99+" past 99). `SmartphoneUI.UpdateBadgeCount` raises `OnUpdatedBadgeCount`, which the pager already hears.
- The game's cancel key is `PlayerAction.Cancel` (`CancelButtonHandler.HandleEscapeClick`, from `GameManager`). With only the HUD up the game opens its menu on it; with a folder open the key is taken first (`[DefaultExecutionOrder(-31000)]`, `PlayerAction.Cancel.Reset`, as `TravelListKeys` does), and left to the game while anything it closes first is up (a question, the menu, the full menu, the map, the options, an activity's panel, a dialogue, a building's card, a preview, the interior designer, a list window, a field being typed in).

## Cost

Nothing per frame. The pager works on the events it worked on before, and on a folder's tile, a move and the option. The view is one pass over a few dozen buttons (`PhoneLayoutRules.View`, no allocation), the order one more; a folder's small pictures are written only where one changed. The tiles are made once a grid, the first time folders are shown. The cancel key's component is switched on only while a folder of the HUD phone is open: one look at the key a frame then.

## Checks

ContractCheck (`PhoneLayoutChecks.cs`): every app of the mod's has a usual place, a folder or the dock, and the dock holds at most two; every button is in exactly one place (the home screen, the dock or one folder's page) for thousands of layouts, orders and mixes of the game's, the mod's and other mods' buttons, with a folder's tile on the home screen exactly while it has an app; the game's own apps first and in order, other mods' after; the pages' tiles with and without the dock and on a folder's page, the More apps button never on an item's tile; the empty tiles; the layout's line read and written back (moves, an app this version lacks, a later version's folder, a later version's line, the most moves); the option off is the plain pages; the folders' numbers; the timing and allocation; the pager's and the tiles' sources; the texts in the three languages and What's new.

In the game: Options > Tests > "Check the phone's folders" writes `Phone check (folders)` for both phones (every button in exactly one place, for the home screen and every folder), and "Open a folder on the phone" opens the HUD phone's first folder with an app. With diagnostics on, opening or closing a folder writes the same check, and a move writes `Phone check (move)`.

## The Contacts app's groups

The game's Contacts app already filters its list by a row of category buttons (`ContactCategorySelection`: one button for each category some contact is in, with the unread badge of its contacts, a tooltip, and the list below in the game's order: unread first, then by the last message). Its seven categories are the numbers 0 to 6 of `ContactCategoryName`. The mod adds seven more, numbers 101 to 107, and puts each of its contacts in one, so the buttons, badges, "Mark all as read" and the filter are the game's own.

- **The groups** (`ContactGroup`): Family, Friends and people, Work and careers, Services and trades, Money and banks, City and government, Other. A group shows only when a contact is in it. The game's own contacts (stores, banks, employees, rivals, Uncle Fred and the rest) stay in the game's categories.
- **The one registry** is `ContactCatalogue.All` (Phone/Contacts): each entry is a contact's id, its picture and its `Group`. A contact with no group fails CatalogueCheck, as does a contact made by a call the registry does not know. Two kinds have no fixed id and are found by their description: the boss of a job (`reallife_contact_boss_desc`, Work) and somebody met (`reallife_social_desc_...`, Friends). A "reallife-" id not in the catalogue is put in Other at run time.
- **How a contact gets its group.** `ContactGroupsGame.Install` runs at each load after the mod's contacts are made: it makes the app's two events (`ContactsApp.onContactAdded` and `onContactRemoved`) if the game has not, because the game sets them to null when it starts and makes them only in the Contacts app's Awake, so the first city of a session finds the app asleep and no events (Continue and Load are the one call, `LoadGame.Load`; the report of 27 Oct 2026 was the first load of a session); it adds the seven `ContactCategory` objects to the row's list (`categories`, a line of the game contract, with the game's own `Icon-` pictures), puts the `ContactGroupsRow` component on the row, listens to `ContactsApp.onContactAdded` and sweeps the phone's contacts. A contact added later is put in its group by the same sweep. The sweep also keeps `PlayerDefaults.contactsLastCategoryName` right, or the app would open on an empty list.
- **The row.** One frame after the game builds its buttons, `ContactGroupsRow` writes the count of contacts into each button's tooltip (a second line, `reallife_contacts_group_count`), gives the mod's buttons the colour of the game's own pictures, and, when more buttons stand in the row than its width holds (the game's seven and the mod's seven are fourteen), makes the whole row smaller by the same factor with its width larger, so the footprint is the game's (`ContactGroups.RowNeeds`, `ScaleFor`). It has no Update.
- **The names** are the game's own keys, `contactcategoryname_101` to `_107` (the game asks for `"contactcategoryname_" + category`).
- **Search** looks through every group: while words are typed the list is the whole list, and the category that was shown comes back when the box is emptied (`ContactSearch`).
- **The option** "Groups in the Contacts app" (`phone_contact_groups`, Phone section, on): off, every contact of the mod has the category it had before groups (General; the BizMan desk Business; a boss Employees) and the buttons go at once.
- **The test**: Options > Tests > "Check the Contacts app's groups" opens the Contacts app and writes `Contact groups check (...)` lines: each button lists exactly the contacts of its category, the buttons together list every contact (nothing lost), every contact of the mod is in its registry group, and typing each name in the search box finds that contact.
- **The save.** The mod writes nothing of its own: the category number is the game's field on the contact, and the mod sets it again at every load, so an old save loads and is grouped. Loaded without the mod, a save that kept the numbers lists those contacts under All only (no button holds them); "Prepare this save for removing the mod" removes them as before.

## Notifications that pop up: a switch for each kind

Late in a game the mod's notifications can come one on another, and each one the game pops up costs a little: a copy of its toast template, the text laid out, a sound, two fades and a coroutine (`NotificationsUI.ShowNotification`, `PlayNotification`). The section "Notifications that pop up" (under Your character and home, after Your phone) has a switch for each kind of the mod's notifications, all on at first: driving and the road, money, property, work, your businesses, the tech sector, the news, family and friends, and the rest of the mod's. The kinds are the ones that give a notification its picture (`NoticeKinds`).

- **A kind switched off** goes straight into the game's notification list without popping up: no toast is made, no sound plays. The list holds it as the game would have written it (its type, key, words and time in `GameInstance.notifications`), whatever its sender asked: a sender keeps a toast out of the list because it was seen, and one kept back was not, so the list is where it is found. The list gets a copy of the words of one its sender kept out (`NoticeMuteRules.ListData`), as the sender may use them again; only a click it carried is lost, as the list has none. One with the duplicate id of a toast that is up is dropped, as the game drops it.
- **Always pop up**, whatever the switches say (`NoticeMuteRules`): the police's and health's notifications, which have no switch (an arrest, a warrant, a fine, a court, prison, a scheme's inquiry; an illness, the doctor, the hospital); every warning and error; the bills, debts and taxes (every power, water and internet toast, the card's statement, debts long overdue, a company's tax return, the year's trading tax, a fine or a ticket the city's policy changed); a death or a retirement in the family and the estate passing on; the morning line with the night's news, which holds only kinds that pop up; what the mod says of itself in answer to the player (the options page's replies, `OptionsNotice`: a preset, an import, an export, a reset, the set-up check, the bug report, a test's answer and a test that needs a city; the Guide, Settings and Staff apps when they cannot open at once; the bug report waiting for a menu to close; the performance tools; the texts service and its AI services; Going Public's word on the session, whose city it is, and why something waits while a session is on; which other mod has the camera; an app moved on the phone; the phone's and the windows' layout put back) and its welcome after an update; the driving test's own words; a reminder the player set in the Planner, and the Planner's welcome; the tests' answers (a key with `_test_`). What a test stages in the city (an event, a shop's stock, a ride) goes as its kind goes, as it would in play. The game's own notifications and other mods' are never touched.
- **The morning line.** While the player sleeps, a notification of a kind switched off is not held for the morning line (`MorningNews.Show`): it goes straight into the list at once.
- **How.** While a kind is off, a gate (`NoticeMute`) stands in the game's toast maker's own place on `Notifications.onShow`: the maker's entry (`NotificationsUI.ShowNotification`, private, a line of the game contract, part `notice_mute`) is found in the event's list by equality and the gate put at its index, so every other listener keeps its order (BikeNames first, NoticeLook and the kit's cards after). The gate hands every notification on to the maker unchanged but one kept back. With every kind on, the gate is not there and the event is the game's own. It is placed behind the loading screen with NoticeLook's look at the toast maker, again when a switch changes or the options are read again (a preset, an import, a reset), once more at the first notification if the maker joined its event late, and taken off with the city. The gate tells the listeners after the maker (NoticeLook, the kit's cards) which notification it kept back (`NoticeMute.KeptBack`), so they leave the newest toast, an older notification's, alone, and still draw one that was made before the gate stood.
- **Faults.** The gate guards itself: a fault in it is counted by its guard and the notification still goes to the maker, which the gate calls outside its guard as the event would. Stood down by the guard, or with the maker gone with its city, the gate gives the maker its place back.
- **Cost.** Nothing per frame. With every kind on, nothing at all; with one off, a notification costs its kind's table walk and a few dozen comparisons, with nothing allocated, and one kept back costs far less than the toast it replaces (one its sender kept out of the list, a copy of its words).
- **Going Public.** The switches are each player's own options; the gate is on this machine's event only, nothing is sent and nothing of the mod's own is saved (one kept back goes into the game's own list on this machine). Going Public's relay is untouched: its patch on `Notifications.Show` still hears every notification, kept back or not, and sends on what it sends. A notification of the mod's that Going Public relays to this machine goes as this player's switches say, and one kept back goes into this machine's list, as any kept back does.
- **The test.** Options > Tests > "Send one notification of each kind and see which pop up" sends the options page's eleven samples 1.2 s apart, then says how many popped up and how many went straight into the list, and writes `Notice mute test: ... PASS` when what the gate kept back is what the switches say and the game's maker made a toast for each sample but those kept back (it counts the toasts in the game's stack). With "Developer diagnostics" on, `Notice check (mute)` says where the gate stands and the first notification it kept back.
- **Checks.** EconomyCheck `--notice-mute` (`NoticeMuteChecks.cs`, also in the full run): each kind's notification kept back by its own switch only; the police's, health's and what is not the mod's under every one of 4,096 sets of kinds off; warnings, what is owed, the morning line's news and the mod's answers with every kind off; every notification the sources raise (over a thousand keys), read by its key as written at the call or through a constant, a variable of the method or a carrier's caller (`Licences.Toast`, `PerfDirector.Notify`), none kept back by another kind's switch and most calm ones able to be; nothing the options page, the mod's apps and tools, Going Public, the shared city, the camera's handover, the performance tools, the texts service, the driving test or the Planner's reminders say kept back, the options page's replies (`OptionsNotice.TextKey`) among them; the list's copy of a sender's words; the cost and the allocation; as code: the game's maker called for every notification but one kept back, each switch read for its own kind, the list record whatever the sender asked, placing, waiting for and taking off the gate, the morning line's question, the options page and the texts. CatalogueCheck NoticeCheck: a scheme's notifications are the police's.
