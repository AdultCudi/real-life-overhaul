# The district save

## In a sentence

Everything in the save that names one of the mod's own places (its district, its streets, the buildings it makes) is moved into the mod's own section of the save at every save and put back right after, and at every load, so the game's own part of the save never names them and a save loads without the mod at any time: the district and what was in it are then simply gone, and come back with the mod.

## Where the code is

- Folder: [`Scripts/DistrictSave`](../../Assets/Mods/RealLifeOverhaul/Scripts/DistrictSave/).
- Read first: `DistrictPlaces.cs` (how a feature names its places), `DistrictSaveDirector.cs` (the timing), `DistrictSaveGame.cs` (every field of the save that can name a place).

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `DistrictPlaces` | the features' places: whole streets, addresses, buildings with their doors, districts, ground and the ways out to the game's streets (game-free: EconomyCheck runs it) |
| `DistrictSaveRules` | whose a place is, what each kind of record does, the moves that take records out of a list or a queue and put them back where they stood, the game's line that a save is written (game-free) |
| `DistrictSaveStore` | the line in modData and its round trip (game-free) |
| `DistrictSaveCodec` | the records to text and back with the game's own serializer |
| `DistrictSaveGame` | the one part that touches the game's records: `.Records` (whole records), `.Elements` (elements of the game's records), `.StandIns` (fields), `.Restore` (the load) |
| `DistrictSaveScan` | the self-check of what is about to be written |
| `DistrictSaveDirector` | the save's step, the put back, the two load passes, the save tools' removal; `.Tests` the test entry |
| `DistrictSaveWatch` | the frame: a put back the game's line did not bring, the waiting log lines, the save hook kept last |
| `DistrictSaveEntry` | the main menu's entry, for the unload that comes after a save is read and before its city is built |

## How a feature names its places

This is the one way in, and it stays as it is. A feature that makes buildings, streets or a district of the mod's registers once, in its director's `Awake` or its static set-up (registering again under the same name replaces the first):

```csharp
DistrictPlaces.Add("uptown", set =>
{
    set.District("reallife:district_uptown");                    // a district of the mod's own
    set.Street("reallife:street_uptown_broadway");               // every number on a street of the mod's own
    set.Address("ba:street_thirteenthstreet", 905);              // or one address: a number no game building uses on a game street
    set.Door("reallife:street_provinglane", 1, x, y, z, facing, "ba:neighborhood_midtown");   // a building of the mod's and the spot outside its door
    set.Ground(-2600f, -1700f, -2300f, -1400f);                  // its own ground, for the player and the cars on it
    set.Exit(-2290f, 0.1f, -1550f, 270f, "ba:neighborhood_hamptons");   // a spot on a game street outside, and the game's district it is in
}, early: PutUptownIntoTheGamesLookups);
```

- **Ids.** The mod's street and district ids start with `reallife` and a separator (`reallife:`, `reallife_` or `reallife-`; `DistrictSaveRules.IsModId`). No game id has that prefix, and no future update's or DLC's can, so a place of the game's own (the Midtown Mall, the JFK area, a second city) is never taken for the mod's. Every record that names a `reallife` street or district leaves the game's part of the save whether or not a feature named it; naming them still matters, for the self-check's line and for what counts as made this session.
- **What each call names.** `District(id)`: every record that names the district (its stats, the market's events there, the products' demand there, a car parked there, a pricing manager watching it). `Street(id)`: every address on it. `Address(street, number)`: one address. `Door(street, number, x, y, z, facing, gameDistrict)`: a building of the mod's at that address and the spot outside its door, on the street it fronts, facing out, where a save made inside it puts the player (and a car inside it). `Ground(x1, z1, x2, z2)`: a rectangle of the mod's own ground in world metres, corners in any order. `Exit(x, y, z, heading, gameDistrict)`: a spot on a game street outside every place of the mod's, where the player and the player's cars out of doors on that ground are written, and the game's district a car is then parked in. Every record of the save that names one of these is handled by its kind (below and `SavePlaces.txt`): a business's registration with everything in it, its staff, candidates, tasks, transactions, contracts, deliveries, fees, deeds and plans, the district's stats and demand, and the stand-ins.
- **The fill** names what the feature makes, from its own data alone: the same on every machine, nothing of the game's asked, no DLC asked. It is called when a save has been read (before its city loads), again at every city load (the district save's step is the last of the city's steps, after every feature has made its places), and before the next save after `DistrictPlaces.Changed()`. A feature that is switched off, or stands down in a city it does not know, names nothing: its records then stay in the mod's section, written again at every save, until a load where it makes its places. Once a load has put any records back, every save of that session takes out what names the mod's places even if no feature names anything any more.
- **When a record goes back at a load.** A kept record goes back only once the game knows every place it names: each address by `BuildingHelper.GetBuilding(new Address(street, number))` not null (the game's own address lookup), each district by `NeighborhoodHelper.Neighborhoods` holding its id. It goes back where it stood (its index in its list), unless the game's part of the save holds one with the same address or id already: then the kept one is let go.
- **The early step** (optional) runs when a save has been read and before its city's scenes are built: the main menu's unload. A feature whose buildings take part in the game's simulation (registrations in the save's list, the game's lookups built from them as the scenes load) puts its buildings' records into the game's address lookup there, and its district into the game's list of districts, so that their records go back before the game builds its lookups from the save's lists (its staff by id, each business's customers, the rivals' lists, the to-do list).
- **Without an early step** the records go back at the district save's city step, the last of the city's: the feature makes its buildings in a city step before it, and nothing may ask the game for those buildings' registrations before the district save's step (`BuildingHelper.GetBuildingRegistration` makes an empty registration and adds it to the save when it finds none, and the kept one is then let go). After that pass the staff lookup, the customers (through `Core/CustomerDraw`) and the rivals' lists are brought up to date.
- **The player and the cars.** The player in a building of the mod's is written outside its door, facing out, with no current address, and the save loads with the player there with or without the mod: nothing of it is kept. A door on the mod's own ground is kept as a spot there, and the save names the way out nearest it. The player and the player's cars out of doors on the mod's ground are written at the way out nearest; without a way out, at the first one named; with none, the position stays and the self-check says so.

## At a save

`SaveGameManager.Save` raises the game's save event, then writes the whole save synchronously, then compresses it on a thread. The mod's one handler in that event (Core/SaveHook) runs after the game's own handlers (it is put last again within a frame of anything that joins the event) and, as its last step after every feature has written its lines, calls the district save:

1. Every record that names one of the mod's places leaves the game's lists (each with its index), or its field is written as a stand-in:
   - **A business's records leave with it**: the registration (with its items, rota and shifts, designs, layout, rival owners, poached staff), its staff and candidates, its tasks, transactions, contracts, deliveries, licence fees, deeds, the district's stats and market events, and the plans of an office there (with their insurance offers). What leaves because something else left (a plan whose manager worked there, a task about an employee there, a message whose button acts on them) carries that one's places, so it goes back only with it.
   - **Elements leave the game's own records, which stay**: a campaign by an agency there, a game business's staff from there, shifts of someone who left, a day's accounts' lines for a business there, a product's demand in the mod's district, a staying plan's entries for a store, a destination or a warehouse there, an HR manager's people who left, a rival's moves against them.
   - **The player and the player's cars, contacts and job stand in**: the player outside a building's door or at the way out (no current address, as a new game starts), a car parked in the way out's game district, a contact with no address, no map destination, no delivery job under way; the real values go back after the write, and at a load where their place is made (the player in a building excepted, above). The save's cars are this player's alone (another Going Public player's car is not in the save).
2. The mod's section is written: `reallife:district_save` (below).
3. The self-check reads what is about to be written (below).
4. The records go back where they stood, the same objects in the same order, the moment the game logs that its file is written ("Game … saved in N ms", or that it could not be): while the save call is still running, so nothing of the game's runs on the shortened lists (the midnight save is made inside the game's hour, before the next hour's work). The put back is armed the moment anything is taken out. If that line never comes, the frame after, or at the next save at the latest.

With no places of the mod's, nothing kept from the load and nothing put back by it, nothing at all is done: one test (`DistrictSaveRules.Idle`, checked to allocate nothing).

## One owner of every record

New buildings has its own save guard (`MadeGame.Save`, from before this framework), which takes out the player inside a made building, the player's destination, a mission, the day's delivery addresses, the food offers and registrations that name a made address, and puts them back a frame later. Two movers of the same records would undo each other, so there is one: the district save. New buildings names every made building standing to it, with the spot outside its door (`MadeGame.NamePlaces`), and its guard steps aside while the district save is present (`DistrictSaveDirector.Present`: true once the district save's city step is done, false from a save's reading until then; the save tools' stop in a city that stays leaves it true). Everything the guard took out is the district save's now, with the same stand-ins in the game's part (the player outside the door, nothing named for the rest), and the rest is kept in the mod's section to come back with the mod instead of being lost at the next load.

The guard does its own only in a city whose district save step failed. Each of the two takes out only what is still there and puts back only what it took, the later taker first (the district save's put back comes on the game's line, the guard's a frame later), so even then nothing is moved twice and every record comes back once, where it stood. EconomyCheck (`DistrictSaveChecks.cs`, both orders, present or not) and ContractCheck (the guard's step aside, `Present` set last in the city step and cleared at a save's reading, the put back armed at once) hold it; under the game's own Mono both movers ran on a copy of a real save with a made building's records in either order, present and not, and every list held the same objects in the same order after.

## At a load

A save chosen from the main menu is read, the menu's mods are unloaded, and only then are the city's scenes built (UI.Load/LoadingScreen). The district save has two passes:

1. **At the main menu's unload** (`DistrictSaveEntry`): the features' early steps run, then every kept record whose places the game knows goes back where it stood. GameManager.Awake, the controllers' Start and TasksUI.Start then see it as the game's own.
2. **At the city load** (the last of the mod's city steps, after every feature has made its places, and before the game puts the player in the city and starts its guides and cars): the rest whose places are made now go back, the game's lookups built from the lists are brought up to date (the staff by id, each business's customers through Core/CustomerDraw, the rivals' lists), and the save hook joins the game's save event after the game's own handlers, before the clock runs. A load that did not pass the main menu (a funeral's backup) does everything here.

A record whose places are still not made stays kept in the mod's section, written again at every save; a stand-in whose place is not made stands (the player and the cars are where the save put them). A record of a kind this version does not know, and one the game's part of the save holds already (the same address, the same id), never goes back. A line this version cannot read (a later version's, or damaged) is kept as it was read under `reallife:district_save_unread` for the version that can.

## The mod's section

One line under `reallife:district_save`: `v=1^r:records^p:payload`. Each record is its kind's code (`DistrictSaveRules`; it never changes), its index, the game's record it stood in (for an element), the mod's addresses and districts it names, its place in the payload, and a stand-in's real values. The payload is the records themselves, written by the game's own serializer exactly as `SaveGameSerializationHelper` writes a save (Odin's binary format under the game's save policy) and read back through the game's own reader of older saves (its migrations of renamed types and enums), then gzip and base64.

Why not the mod's line store: a registration is some fifty fields deep (items with their cargo, the rota, 300 interior designs in a large shop, orders, staff), and any field a game update adds would be lost. Measured under the game's own Mono with the game's assemblies, on a real save of the owner's: its largest shop (a gym with 188 items and 306 interior designs) is 299 KB of Odin, 37 KB gzipped, 50,000 base64 characters, about 5 ms to write and 26 ms to read; ten shops 52 KB gzipped, 7 ms to write. The self-check line gives the size and time of every save.

The whole way through, on a copy of that save (885 registrations) with the gym named a place of the mod's: the save step took out 30 records (the registration, 20 transactions, 7 days' lines of the accounts, a task, a delivery contract) and stood in for a car parked there, in 18 ms; the self-check over the whole save took 9 ms (17 ms the first time in a session) and found the gym named nowhere; the mod's section was 50,036 characters, 27 ms to write the first time in a session and 21 ms to read. The file written by the game's own writer and read by its own reader, as a game without the mod reads it, named the gym nowhere; the load put all 31 back, and the registrations in order with their items and designs, the transactions, the tasks, the deliveries, each day's lines and the cars were the same as before the save.

A district of many buildings in the game's simulation, the same way: whole streets of that save named the mod's, with 142 registrations of the game's own businesses (154 records with what names them), took 12 ms to take out the first time in a session and under 1 ms after, 21 to 23 ms to write the section (122,967 characters), 8 to 14 ms for the self-check, and 41 ms to read back at a load; with 256 registrations (269 records), 26 to 28 ms to write (172,781 characters) and 60 ms to read. The game's own write of the whole save is 15 ms on the main thread (its log says so at every save), and its compression runs on a thread of its own. A business of the player's own adds about what the gym does.

## The guards

- **Offline, every gate (ContractCheck, "the save's fields that can name a place of the mod's").** It walks every type reachable from `GameInstance` in the game's assemblies on disk (lists, dictionaries, tuples, every subclass of a field's type), by the game's own rule of what is written, and lists every field that can hold an address, a street and its number, a district, a position (the player's, a car's), or an id of someone or a plan that leaves with a business. The list must be exactly [`ContractCheck/SavePlaces.txt`](../../RealLifeOverhaul.Build/ContractCheck/SavePlaces.txt), where each line says what the district save does with the field. A field a game update or a DLC adds (a new list of businesses, a second city's records) fails the gate by name. It also fails when a field the list says is handled has no `// save path:` line in `Scripts/DistrictSave`, when the game starts to read or write `GameInstance.modData` (today it only makes it, in its own initializer), when SaveHook's last step is no longer the district save's, when the save tools no longer leave its key or no longer take the places out for good through it, when new buildings' save guard no longer steps aside while it is present, and when anything of the mod's writes a save other than through the game's `SaveGameManager.Save`.
- **At every save (the self-check).** After the records have left, every value the game's serializer is about to write is gone through by reflection, along a plan made once a session per type by the same rule (a type that cannot hold a place below it, such as a shop's items, is never entered): every address, every street and district by its field's name, every reference to someone or a plan that left, and the player and the cars on the mod's ground. A field a game update added is gone through like the others, so a place of the mod's in it is found before the mod knows the field. With Developer diagnostics on: `District save check: the written save names none of the mod's N addresses (S whole streets) and M districts (…): PASS`; anything still named is always logged, as `… still names the mod's … at <path> = <value>: FAIL`.

## Going Public

- Each machine writes its own save with `SaveGameManager.Save`, and the host serves it back at a join as the file's bytes: the mod's section travels in the file, and each machine puts back its own player's records. Nothing new is sent over the network.
- Going Public strips its own records around every save (a Harmony prefix and finalizer on `Save`, and around its own session saves): it takes them out before the save event and puts them back after `Save` returns, appending what is missing. The district save takes its records out inside the save event and puts them back on the game's line, before `Save` returns: the two are nested, neither sees the other's records, and Going Public's put back appends, so neither undoes the other in either order.
- Another player's business mirrored into this world (a rival-owned registration of a session player's) that stands at a place of the mod's is taken out for the write and put back after it, never kept: Going Public mirrors it again at a join. The save tools' removal leaves it where it stands.
- Deterministic: no randomness; the places are the features' fixed data; nothing depends on the DLCs a player owns.

## In another city, and when its feature is away

The district save has no feature of its own and never stands down: every save in any city goes through it. A New York feature that makes places stands down in another city (Core/CityCheck), names nothing there, and its records stay in the mod's section until the player is back in New York.

## The save tools

"Prepare this save for removing the mod" and "Reset the mod's data" leave the district's keys: they hold the player's own things, and a save loads without the mod with them there. The removal stops the mod in a city that stays, so after the features have stopped and before its save it asks the district save to take every record that names the mod's places out of the save in memory for good (`KeepOutForGood`), with the places as the city loaded them (a made building's door is still known), kept in the mod's section; the stand-ins are written for good, and another player's things stay where they are. Whatever the player does next in that city, no save names those places, and they come back with the mod. If the section cannot be written then, nothing is taken out for good and every save goes on taking them out and putting them back. The removal tool's report says how many records stay.

## After a game update

Run the gate. When "the save's fields that can name a place of the mod's" fails on a new field: read what it holds and what writes it; handle it in `Scripts/DistrictSave` (a whole record that leaves, an element of a game record, or a stand-in, with a `// save path:` line); give it a line in `SavePlaces.txt` with its kind's code; run the gate again. A field gone: take its line out and its handling. The same list is the thing to read when a DLC adds a city: its records are the game's own and never the mod's (no `reallife` id), and they need nothing unless they can name a place of the mod's.

## What the parts that make places connect at the merge

- Register with `DistrictPlaces.Add` (a fill, and an early step if its buildings take part in the game's simulation); call `DistrictPlaces.Changed()` when its places change in play.
- Make the buildings' city objects in a city load step before the district save's (its step is the last of `RealLifeOverhaulCity.OnLoadAsync`), or make their records known to the game in the early step.
- New buildings is wired: its made buildings and their doors are named in `NewBuildingsDirector.Awake` (`MadeGame.NamePlaces`), and its save guard steps aside (above). A made building today keeps an empty registration of its own in the game's private lookup and none in the save's list; a feature that lets the player rent one hands the made building the registration the district save keeps for its address (`DistrictSaveDirector.KeptRegistration(street, number)`, read when the building is made), not a new empty one, and leaves putting it into the save's list to the district save: the same object goes back where it stood at the city step (one a feature put there itself, this very object, counts as back).
