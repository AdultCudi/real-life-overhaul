# The city's population

## In a sentence

Under the living economy's master option **Living population**, every flat in the city's residential buildings is a home with a household of real people who age, study, work, pair up, have children, move and die; they earn, pay rent and tax and spend at the businesses within reach, and every business's customers (the player's, the city's and the rivals') follow them from the game's own count on the business's first day.

## Where the code is

- Folder: [`Scripts/Population`](../../Assets/Mods/RealLifeOverhaul/Scripts/Population/); the wiring in `Scripts/Wiring/Wiring.PopulationLinks.cs`; the mod's copies of the game's customer arithmetic read `Scripts/Core/CustomerScale.cs`; Uptown's demand reads `Scripts/Uptown/DemandBridges.cs`.
- What the people bring the mod's own businesses: [`Scripts/Population/Demand`](../../Assets/Mods/RealLifeOverhaul/Scripts/Population/Demand/), its timing in `PopulationDirector.Demand.cs`, each business's bridge wired in `Scripts/Wiring/Wiring.DemandLinks.cs`.
- Option: "Living population" (`living_population`, the overhaul's master switch, on in the beta; the feature "living" owns it). Off, the population stands down: the game's own customers, the city economy's own district incomes, the Economy app's tables by cycle and Uptown's fixed ratios stand, exactly as before.
- The design: `not-in-repo/plans/tech-overhaul/POPULATION.md` (with the owner's answers of 1 Nov 2026).

## The measure

The owner chose the bigger city (1 Nov 2026): 18,000 to 22,000 households and 50,000 to 65,000 people. A home is sized as a real flat, not as the game's unit: a residential building's first flat is the game's own unit (the one it lets, at its size and rent), and the rest of its floor times 0.82 net is flats of its district's size, 52 m2 in the Garment District, 54 in Industry City, 56 in Lower Manhattan, 66 in Hell's Kitchen, 78 in Murray Hill and 88 in Midtown (New York's average rented flat is some 65 to 70 m2 net); Uptown's and Cedar Heights' flats are the mix of their classes' (52, 68 and 90 m2). A Hamptons house is one household. The homes are the game's residential buildings alone, Build 3682's 363 and Uptown's and Cedar Heights' 222, which stand from the first day of a game (the band was filled on 23 Oct 2026): 20,638 flats, 69 m2 net on average, 7,475 of them in the band; generated, some 19,700 households and 56,300 people. Before 2 Nov 2026 a share of the shops' upper floors was counted as homes too, sized so that Build 3682's city alone held the owner's size; with the band's buildings beside it the betas' cities came to 28,500 households and 81,600 people (POPULATION.md section 16). The checks measure the city the game lists at a load: Build 3682's 885 buildings and the band's 541 as the beta's load declared them (EconomyCheck/PopulationCity.txt), and fail if the city it generates leaves the owner's size; with Developer diagnostics on, the load says the same in its line "Population check (the city's size)". Jobs: an office a desk for every 16 m2 of its net floor, a shop by its sales floor and kind, a warehouse by its floor, a cinema 30 and a theatre 60, a special service by its kind's density, a residential building a member of staff for every 40 of the game's units: 72,663 in Build 3682's buildings and 37,916 in the band's, most in its office towers, 110,579 in all. As Uptown builds, its homes add to the city.

## The people

Generated from the buildings with the city economy's seed (or a save's own), a flat at a time from a hash of the seed and its address: 4.3% of flats left free (the share its first years settle at as grown children and parting couples take flats; the market then keeps 5% for the region's arrivals), the household's class from its district's shares (the game's own unit leaned by its size), its size by its class (about 2.8 people a household: drawn at 3.2 for the working class, 2.8 for the middle and 2.5 for the upper, and no more people than the flat holds at 9 m2 each), its kind (couples with children, lone parents, three generations, flatmates, people alone), ages, schooling, work, tastes, savings, debt, cars, and whether it owns its flat. A generated tenant the market's rent would drive out holds a regulated tenancy, as about half of New York's renters do; they are still needed in the bigger city (without them some 600 households, 3%, leave in the first twelve days). Somebody brings money in wherever somebody stays home: an adult at home of working age in a household with nobody in work, looking for work or on a pension looks for work instead, when the city is generated, when a household comes from the region, forms, parts or loses a member. Savings and card debt follow the Federal Reserve's Survey of Consumer Finances 2022 by usual income (the pay of those in work, between jobs or retired) and the head's age: savings are the survey's financial assets, a median of 25 to 1,187 days of usual income from the poorest fifth to the richest tenth, leaned from about a third of that for heads under 35 to three and a half times at 65 to 74, drawn between a quarter and four times that; about 45% carry a card balance of some days' income (car, study and other loans are paid in the month's spending); and every household has its first month's rent and deposit at least (60 days of rent), so a first rent puts nobody in debt. EconomyCheck's figures for the city the game lists (with Uptown and Cedar Heights): 19,798 households and 56,707 people (2.86 a household, 21% alone; 28% under 18 and 9% over 65), 27,037 residents in work and 81,055 commuters (73% of 110,792 jobs: in 2017 71% of Manhattan's workers lived outside it); savings a median 314 days of usual income, 48% with a card balance of a median 10 days.

The population keeps the living economy's one clock (`CalendarRules.MoneyYearDays`'s note): its years are the save's, as the game ages its own staff by them, and a month is a twelfth of the save's year, so its life goes alike at any year length. The region's commuters fill a share of the open jobs a month and its migrants come by the month, as the residents' own job finding is a rate of the save's year (by the day they would fill a 365-day city's jobs six times as fast as its people find work, with unemployment at 13% and every field's pay at its floor within three years).

Each day: birthdays move people through school, university, work and retirement at 65; couples form, have children and part; grown children leave home for a flat of their own; people die by their age. Each month: rents move with vacancy, 1.2% a year for each point of it off its target of 5% (the pace rent surveys find; four times that would take two districts to the index's bound in six years); households move when the rent is past what they bear or the flat gives a person less than 13 m2, choosing a flat by its room, by a standard that fits what they would pay (its rent at the game's own prices against some 28% of their income), by what the district's index adds as price alone, by its class and by the way to work; households from the region take free flats while jobs are open, fewer as the district's rents rise past the game's (a thousand over the index, squared); of those who want another flat and find none, 6% a month take one in the region, times the index squared; some leave after a long time out of work. So a district's rents rise until enough go and few enough come, then hold: in the base city the working-class districts settle some 35 to 40% above the game's rents within eight years and stay there, and the market never reaches its bounds (650 to 1,800, kept as guards). Over ten years without the player and without new homes the city goes from 56,200 to 58,300 people and keeps 19,500 households, with about thirteen births and six deaths a thousand a year, and unemployment of 5 to 6%; through the 61 of its years a 60-day calendar lives in ten years of days it keeps within a tenth of its first day's people.

## Jobs and money

Every job belongs to an employer: a business of the game's, the other firms of an office building, the public sector, a special service, a building's staff. Residents fill them by field and distance; commuters from the region take the rest, fewer while the city's own people are out of work (employers take those who live near first: at twice the start rate of unemployment the region fills a fifth of what it would). The player's businesses employ the game's own staff, each linked to a resident. Pay is the game's scale (12 to 18 an hour unskilled, more with skill and in the professions), and each month a field's pay moves part of the way to its wage curve's level (`LabourRules.WageTarget`): about 7% lower where twice the city's usual share of its people are looking and as much higher where none are, within 860 to 1,200 thousandths of its start, so it falls while its people look and comes back as they find work (a pay that only fell, a percent a month, would rest on its floor of 800 within a few years).

A household's day: pay, benefits, pensions and the returns on its savings in (an annual rate over 365 days whatever the save's year: the savings are money, made in days of pay and saved by the day); income tax, rent, its bills (utilities and what no business of the city takes: insurance, health care, pension contributions, schooling) and about twenty kinds of spending by its income against the city's middle and its tastes; the rest saved, about a twentieth of what is left for the middle and a fifth for the top, as the Consumer Expenditure Survey's households save once those bills are counted. Each household keeps a ledger of the running month and the last: income by source, taxes, rent and to whom, spending by kind, saving and debt. Spending goes into pools by district, class and kind (at home and near work, and the commuters' and visitors' own), routed to the districts in reach and shared among the businesses there by price against what the class bears, interior, satisfaction, attention and habit. Every cent is placed by whole-number shares, so a household's spending at a business can be worked out afterwards and, summed over households, is that business's takings from residents to the cent.

## Growth

The city grows only on the mod's own land (POPULATION.md section 14; section 15 has every source). Turnover keeps it young: a flat that empties or whose household leaves goes on the month's free list at once and the region's movers, who are young (heads drawn from mover ages peaking at 25 to 34), take it first; households of people all past 65 leave at 2.2% a year (6% from 80), others for elsewhere in the country at 2.6% (head under 45) or 1.4%, twice that with a child of six or under (families with young children leave the city twice as often, the Fiscal Policy Institute finds); a woman without a partner at home has children at a fifth of a partnered woman's rate. Each month the housing pressure (0 to 100) is read from the city's free flats under 7% and the households turned away, and the city wants homes started at up to 0.8% of its homes a year (New York's pace) times the pressure and the share of the mod's land left. That figure is the construction demand: Uptown's and Cedar Heights' homes pull is open while the year's homes are not all started and closed (-100) once they are, so the city's developers start homes only then (they report each start back), and private clients' tenders read the same pull. The office floors' firms (the measured jobs are the let floors; a tenth stood empty) move toward three parts the residents' labour force and one their spending; past the floor's top the jobs still wanted, at most 0.8% of the office jobs a year, open the mod's land's offices pull until they are started. Singles who look for a partner keep looking through the month, so couples form alike at any year length, and the game studio reads the residents who play as a share of the people: the city's tastes, not its size. About 9.5% of firms open and as many close each year, plus the net change. At each month's turn the director reads the city's buildings and binds the table again when one was finished, demolished, extended or converted (any builder's, the player's too), and measures the land left (the construction game's free columns and each standing city home's room under the code). The base districts get no new buildings: their rents, free flats and the estate's conversions answer. The Economy app's city screen shows homes wanted and built and firms opened and closed; the construction company's demand screen the homes wanted, started and built.

## The game's customers

At start the game's five customer calculators are given the mod's own subclasses (the game ships no Harmony, so no patch: the factory keeps its calculators in static fields). Each asks the game's own method for the hour with the initial customers scaled by the business's factor: its takings today over its takings on its first day, with the game's promotion since then taken back out (the population's attention carries it). The staffed furniture's cap, an office's workers on shift, the day and hour factors and the AI's draw stay the game's. On a business's first day nothing moves. AI businesses close by the game's own rule when people go elsewhere. The mod's copies of the arithmetic (the staff rota, the car dealership, the bank branches, a brand's site estimate, the Economy app's change in each business's customers) read the same scale; Meaningful Ambience's count reads the game's calculator, so it has it too. The business features' levers (issues, campaigns, brands, faults, Flare, the interior) no longer write a clamped promotion while the population runs: they are attention that draws people from rivals.

## Games

Each person from six has the platforms they play on (PC, console, phone, by age, class and sex: some three in five people play, most on phones) and the studio's twelve genres they like (by their main platform's share in the game studio's own table, leaned by age and sex). A child first drawn takes some of a parent's likes and plays more on what the home has; each birthday redraws a few bits for the new age. The day's figures count the residents of each genre on each platform, by district and class (`CityPopulation.Players`, `PlayersIn`, `PlayersOfClass`). The game studio's audience (`StudioBridges.Population`, wired in `Wiring.PopulationLinks`) is those residents times the world's people over the residents at the population's start, so its first day is the documented world's (`StudioBridges.World`) and it follows births, deaths, moves and the world's growth after; a guest's is the host's. In the base city 72% of people from six play: 28% on PCs, 30% on consoles, 51% on phones.

## What else reads it

The city economy's district incomes follow the households' mean incomes from where they stood; the Economy app's unemployment and vacancies are the measured market's; Uptown's and Cedar Heights' demand for homes, shops, offices and warehouses comes from the people (households wanting a flat, the region wanting in, takings growing, spending per shop floor, goods per warehouse floor). The council's taxes (income, sales at 8.9% of prices, property at 15% of rents) are an account the civic work will read (`CityPopulation.TaxesMonth`).

## The mod's own businesses

The mod's own businesses keep their own rules and take the number of people they draw on from the population (`Population/Demand`; POPULATION.md section 10). Each day, after the population's own day and in the same slices, `PopDemandJob` measures some twenty sources from the households, people, employers and businesses: the households' savings; their incomes by credit band (the band from their income against the city's middle and their debt against their income, debt past a year's income being default; the population starts with the card balances the Survey of Consumer Finances finds and adds debt only where a household spends past its income, so debt past some weeks' income marks distress and income carries the rest); the businesses' takings and the private wage bill; the visitors the districts draw; residents' nights out; households without a car; households that took a flat in the last month or still look for one, and their budget for rent; owners selling; young households taking a home; the middle and upper class's savings; the day's taxes; the districts' pull for building; households ready to buy a car; online spending by kind (the US Census Bureau's e-commerce shares); households selling second hand and the working and middle class's spending on goods; people who want a course; the households' satisfaction (worked out from their rent, work, savings, debt and room, since no record keeps it); the professions' jobseekers and unfilled posts; adults and households. Each district gets its households, businesses, adults, people, work and income, and leans against the city's: income, saving, debt, the housing market, its borrowers' bands and its crime (income, unemployment and young people weighed).

Each demand is a recipe of sources and weights (`PopDemandCatalogue.Recipes`), read as a factor on the business's old figure: its sources against what they were on the anchor day. A city is anchored on the first day after it has lived through three months' turns (`PopDemandCatalogue.SettleMonths`: its moves and arrivals in its figures, and the wave of households the generation placed in flats that do not suit them behind it), so every factor is a thousand that day and every business keeps today's figures; a city generated again is anchored again. Anchored on the first turn's wave, the estate agency's and the Marketplace's figures would fall to half by the second year. Before the anchor, and with the option off, every factor is a thousand.

- The own bank: the people's savings pool follows the households' savings, the firms' their takings and wage bills, each credit band's borrowing the households in it, shops' and companies' their takings and wage bills; the rival banks hold the rest of the same pools. A branch's visits (what they are for, their sums and who asks for a loan) lean by its district's residents.
- Property: hotel guests (business travel, the visitors the city draws, residents' trips), car hire (visitors and residents without a car), a landlord's applicants (the housing market, leaned by the building's district) and their offers (their budget for rent), the agency's owners (households moving and owners selling).
- The construction company: flat buyers (young households taking a home, the middle and upper class's savings); the days between private clients' and refits' tenders (the districts' pull and the businesses' takings) and council buildings' tenders (the taxes). Rival builders bid on the same tenders.
- The car showroom's buyers, an online shop's orders (its kind's online spending), the Marketplace's sellers and buyers, the academy's students.
- Broadband's market in each district (its homes and businesses against the anchor day's).
- Civic: each district's electorate is its adult residents (the floor space's guess before the population measures it); the council's revenue stands on the floor space's electorate moved by the people's taxes, so it is what it was on the anchor day; the mayor's approval takes the residents' satisfaction (`PopDemandCatalogue.SatisfactionApproval` points for its whole first-day figure); street crime where the player stands and the district map's crime layer lean by each district's crime.
- The labour market: the tech sector's jobseekers follow the professions' unemployed and those looking while in work, the player's openings the professions' vacancies at the city's employers, each within its list's bounds.

The Economy app's screens are view models (`PopDemandViews`): the business types whose demand moved most since the first day, and each type's demands with each source's part. The Guide's page "Customers from the city's people" says where each business's customers come from, and each business's own page ends with a pointer to it while the option is on.

Not yet on the people: the game's own job candidates (the recruitment agency's and the job board's, drawn by the game's code), and broadband in Uptown and Cedar Heights (the company's network has the base city's seven districts).

## The save and Going Public

One line, `reallife:population` (version 3 adds the growth's columns at the end: the land, the pressure and homes, the firms' level and churn; an older line loads with the firms at their start and the land measured at the first month; version 4 ends with the measure its homes were counted by, and a line of an older measure, every beta's, is generated again at its load from the buildings with its own seed, the same city on every machine): binary columns compressed in pieces on the save's workers by the district save's zip, version 2 telling each number against what the rest make it (ids against the one before, days against today, a rent against its flat's, pay against the going rate, the ledger in whole money against the household's day and shares), so the city's line is 1.5 MB in a year of 60 days and 1.8 MB in one of 365, under the 2 MB budget; a line of version 1 is still read, its people's games drawn as it loads; a save without it generates the city; a load binds it to the city as it stands (a household whose building is not standing is kept, away, and looks for a flat). In a session the host owns the people and shares part P (the day, the books, each district's figures, each business's takings by address, the residents of each genre on each platform, and since the growth a `w:` section of homes and firms that an older reader skips); a guest anchors each business on the first figures it hears and lives no population of its own.

The businesses' demand has a small line of its own, `reallife:population_demand` (the anchor and the last day's sources and district figures, so the factors stand the moment a save loads; a line of another population is forgotten). In a session the host adds two sections to part P, each with its own version (`m:` the factors, `n:` each district's homes, businesses, adults and leans); a reader from before them skips them, and a guest's own businesses read the host's figures.

## Checks

EconomyCheck (`--population`): the measure (the owner's band of households and people, flats of about 70 m2, jobs unchanged), the generation (bands, the same seed the same city), a day with every cent placed and every household's part summing to each business's takings, ten years of life, the labour market, the costs a slice, the save's round trip, its size in a year of 365 days and a damaged line, the rebind, the Going Public part, the factor, the games (the studio's table, the first day the world's, the counts the people's as they change), every screen's view model.

EconomyCheck (`--demand`, PopDemandChecks.cs, PopDemandBusinessChecks.cs, PopDemandTextChecks.cs): the day's measure counts every household, person and cent once (the districts sum to the city, the takings and taxes are the pools'); nothing moves before the anchor and every demand reads a thousand on the anchor day; the same seed gives the same factors; ten years keep every factor sane; the save's line and part P carry it whole and an older reader skips it; every screen; the measure within the population's slice; and each business: as before with no population and on the anchor day, its shared pools conserving, its figures leaning the right way and within the money scale over ten years.

The living years (LivingYearsChecks.cs) hold the features together on the living city. The full run reads the demand's ten years as them: every band asserted (the people within a tenth of the first day's and the households within a twentieth; births and deaths at Manhattan's rates; unemployment 2 to 10%; every district's rents within 800 to 1,600 of the game's and none at a bound, the city's on average within 900 to 1,300; every field's pay on its wage curve and none at its floor; digital spending a person within 850 to 1,400 of the first day's; the residents who play within 900 to 1,100; every business's demand within 600 to 1,700 of its anchor day and the savings pools within the own bank's half to twice), the documented city standing for the living one's first day, every tech product's and the studio's payback in its band on the living city and the documented one, and the one clock (a year of 365 days moving the city as the default's first does). `--living-years` runs the same over 3,650 days at years of 60 days (61 of its years, held to the long run's bounds too) and of 365, in about a hundred seconds.
