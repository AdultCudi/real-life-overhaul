# Driving

## In a sentence

The car the player drives gets the performance of its kind (power, gearing and drag worked out from a target per vehicle type), the handling of its kind (weight, a soft ride that swings and settles, body roll and pitch, steering by speed, tyres that slide, brakes that build without locking), an eased throttle, a speed reading in the game's own vehicle panel and turn signals that come on by themselves as it turns.

## Where the code is

- Folder: [`Scripts/Driving`](../../Assets/Mods/RealLifeOverhaul/Scripts/Driving/).
- Read first: `CarCatalogue.cs` (the targets for every vehicle type, game-free), `CarModel.cs` (the physics model that finds power, gearing and drag) and `CarTuning.cs` (applies it to the driven car and puts every value back).

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |

| `CarModel` | a car in a straight line as the game's physics moves it; finds the power, gearing and drag that meet a target (game-free) |
| `CarTuning` | reads the driven car, applies what CarModel found, puts every value back; the eased pedal |

## How it talks to other features

- The Garage's performance parts tune a car through `CarModel` and a hook in `CarTuning` (`Garage/GarageTuning`); see [Garage](garage.md).
- Traffic is never tuned: only the car the player drives.
- The handling lies under the Garage's parts: `DrivingBridges.LiftParts` and `LayParts` (answered in `Wiring.DrivingLinks`) take the custom shop's springs, dampers, bars, weight, brakes and tyres off the car before the handling changes, and put them back on top after. When the Garage lays its parts on the car being driven or takes them off itself (a build bought or put back sitting in it, the road turning wet or dry, the shop's preview, a save loaded), it says so through `GarageBridges.PartsLaid` (filled in by `Wiring.DrivingLinks`), and from the next frame `CarTuning` works out what they change with everything of the mod's left on the car, never as an option changed (`HandlingRules.Frame`, `CarHandling.Relaid`, the parts read in a frame of their own): the parts the handling was made and its brakes shared for change nothing (the road turning wet or dry under a build with no tyres part, the one part the weather changes); otherwise the brake shares and the full pedal held to the front springs' travel are worked out again for the parts at once, and where the design was made for other brakes, weight or tyres (`HandlingRules.Relay`) it is made again from the car as it was read when the player got in, beside the one on the car, which stays on until the new one takes its place in one frame (its wheels and axles; the weight, steering, ground and ABS stay as they are). The power, gearing, drag and eased pedals stay on: the engine is the same on a wet road, and a part bought with car tuning on changes its options (`GarageTuning.Stamp`), which begins everything again.
- Wet roads (`Events/Weather`) and worn tyres and brakes (`Transport/CarOwnership`) scale the tyres' grip and the brakes' torque on top of the handling as before: the handling changes neither figure.

## Cars: performance by kind

The game's cars are all 1400 kg (the Vord Pony 1200), share one four-speed gearbox and one engine curve, have no air drag, and are stopped by a speed limiter at the "max speed" of their vehicle type (50 to 100 km/h).

`Driving/CarTuning` gives the car being driven (never traffic) the performance of its kind instead: each vehicle type has a target in `CarCatalogue`, and `CarModel` works out from the car's own mass, wheels, grip, clutch and power curve the engine power, gear ratios and drag that meet it.

Top speed is where the engine's power in top gear equals drag plus rolling resistance (the limiter is switched off), so a car pulls hard to 50, steadily to 100 and builds slowly to its top speed.
Everything changed is recorded and put back when the player gets out, changes car, switches the option off or the mod unloads.

The "applied" and "model" columns are what `CarModel` gives for the game's own vehicle data (read from the game's files with a small command-line check, at both sliders 100 % and with the eased throttle).
The mod works the same sums out again from the live car each time the player gets in, and writes them to the log once per car ("Car tuning: ...").

They are a model of the physics, not a measurement: the options button "Measure this car" times a real run for comparison.

- **A type the table does not know** (a car from another mod) is placed by `CarCatalogue.Guess`: no fuel tank, carried in the hands or under 600 kg: untouched.
  The game's own max speed up to 55: truck (18 s, 135).
  Up to 65: van (15 s, 145) with cargo room for 14 or more, else economy (12 s, 170).
  Cargo 16 or more: utility (10 s, 180).

  Max speed 95 or more: sports (4.8 s, 270), 85 or more: sports (5.5 s, 250).
  Otherwise luxury (6.5 s, 240) if flagged so, else saloon (9 s, 200).
  To give it its own figures, add one `case` to `CarCatalogue.For`.

- **Cars of other mods with their own row**, by their whole type names (their data is in their own asset files, not read here, so the "applied" and "model" columns are worked out in the game only): Empire Motors' two car add-ons, sold at the game's dealership through that mod's desk (docs/other-mods.md), `empiremustanggt:vehicletype_mustanggt` (Mustang GT, Sports, 4.5 s, 250) and `empirebmwg82:vehicletype_empirebmwg82` (BMW G82, Sports, 4.0 s, 250).

  Without their rows they were placed by the game's own max speed they carry, which the guess cannot be sure of.

- **The two sliders** (70 to 150 %) scale the top speed and divide the 0-100 time.
  Top speed is never taken past 350 km/h; with a top speed turned down below 125 km/h the power is found as if for 125 and the car is then geared and dragged down to what was asked.
- **Gearing**: top gear turns the engine at 95 % of its limit at top speed; first gear runs out at a fifth of top speed (45 to 58 km/h), never so short that the engine's best torque spins the tyres; the gears between are evenly spaced.

  Driven gently the box changes up at 45 to 58 % of the rev limit by kind, with the pedal down at 95 %; a change takes 0.12 s (sports) to 0.45 s (trucks), less with the custom shop's gear change parts (see [Garage](garage.md#performance-parts)).

- **The eased throttle** ("Smooth throttle"): the car's own input handler calls `CarTuning.EasePedal` once a frame, which moves the pedal towards what the key asks: 0 to full in 0.7 s, slowly at first, and back in 0.15 s.
  A tap gives a gentle pull-away; an analogue trigger keeps its own feel.
  The brakes come on over 0.25 s through the physics' own `brakes.actuationTime`; with car handling on, the brake key is eased as well (Handling, below).

  In reverse the game swaps the pedals, and the one that drives is the one eased.

- **Brakes** are left alone by car tuning: with the game's tyres they stop a car from 100 km/h in 19 to 56 m and from its new top speed in 66 to 122 m. Car handling has brakes of its own (below).
- A car the sums cannot be done for (no combustion engine, no automatic gearbox, missing wheels or curve) is left exactly as the game made it, with one line in the log saying why.

## Handling

**What the game's own cars start from** (read from the game's files: each car's prefab and each vehicle type). The cars weigh 1400 kg (the Vord Pony 1200), with the centre of mass 0.2 to 0.4 m above the road (the vans 0.66 and 0.71). Every car's tyres are the game's ArcadeTireFrictionPreset (B 7.09, C 0.87, D 2, E 0.5), grip 1.3 front and 1.5 rear, slip stiffness 1.27 and 1.7: its curve never peaks and reaches 1.84 sideways on, about 4.5 g. The game's ground detection gives the asphalt preset only on a collider tagged "Road", a tag the game does not have, or on one texture of two terrains. The springs run from 5 000 N over 0.6 m to 19 300 N over 0.2 m (0.8 to 2.9 Hz; straight, but for the Mersaidi Dash's rising curve), every damper 5 000 N s/m, 1.4 times that in bump below 0.06 m/s and 1.6 in rebound below 0.05 m/s, 0.6 above: most cars never swing past rest after a bump, the stiffly sprung ones (Ferdinand 112, Mersaidi S500, Vord Pony) swing once. Every axle has a 4 200 N/m anti-roll bar and applies its tyres' forces 0.2 to 0.8 of the spring's length above the road. The rear brakes get 0.184 of the front's torque and there is no ABS, so the fronts of the cars with strong brakes lock, still pulling their most. The steering's lock falls to half (to 0.4 on the vans, trucks and older cars) by 54 km/h and to a fifth by 180 km/h, and the game's Ackermann sum turns the front wheels further than the lock on a short car. Each vehicle type has its own top speed, 50 to 100 km/h. With the key held the game's cars turn hard: the Anselmo AF90 at 2.4 g at 50 km/h and 3.4 g at 100.

**0.39.0 to 0.40.4, and now.** From 0.39.0 the handling replaced those values with figures of its own: tyres holding about 1.1 g (a quarter of the game's) on a slip stiffness of 1, soft springs damped at about 0.26 with no anti-roll bars, the centre of mass raised. A turn the game's car takes at a speed came out about twice as wide, so a player turning as they always had left the road, and the body floated. Now the handling is the car's own game values, read when the player gets in, with a little added (`HandlingModel.Design`):

| what | the game's own, read from the car | Light, Full, Heavy |
|---|---|---|
| weight | the body's mass (`Rigidbody.mass`, `VariableCenterOfMass.baseMass`) and inertia | a share of the way to what its kind weighs (`HandlingRules.KindMass`), 0.1, 0.25 and 0.5, never lighter than the game made it; the inertia scaled with the mass |
| springs, bars, tyres' ratings | each wheel's spring (force x its own curve of the compression), each axle's anti-roll bar, each tyre's load rating | scaled with the weight, so the car stands as high and rides at the same frequency; the springs' curves stay the game's; a stiffer car of another mod is first given the game's softest ride for its kind that its travel at rest carries (below) |
| the centre of mass | `Rigidbody.centerOfMass` | the game's own |
| damping | the game's two-rate dampers | their shape kept; the rates set so that the first swing past rest after a 3 cm push (half the travel if less) gives a damping ratio of 0.55 at Full and 0.42 at Heavy, or the game's own where that is lighter; Light keeps the game's own |
| roll | the force points' height, the springs and the bars | the game's own roll per g and 0.15, 0.4 or 0.8 degrees more, from the force points brought down (15 cm at most, never below 2 cm), as far as rolling over allows |
| nose dive | the force points and the anti-squat figure | the game's own dive per g times 1.05, 1.2 or 1.4, by anti-squat; held back towards the game's own, and the front dampers' compression firmed, where the full pedal would leave the front springs less than a third of their travel (below) |
| the tyres | the car's friction preset and slip stiffness | the game's own curve exactly up to the knee, flat past it: the give at the very limit. The knee lies 15% past the furthest any of the game's own car's tyres goes in its hardest turn that still holds the road (with the key held, at the type's own top speed, or the fastest below it at which the game's car keeps all four wheels down and its front tyres under 20 degrees of slip), and at least at 28, 20 or 16 degrees of front slip angle; the game's slip stiffness and grip; a ground preset with no surfaces of its own, so a terrain never swaps the tyres |
| rolling over | | a sudden turn's swing counted, the car rolls over only at 1.2 times what its tyres hold (the custom shop's grippiest tyres give 1.2): where it would sooner, the tyres give sooner (never before the game's hardest turn), and where even that is not enough the force points go up, which takes some of the roll away |
| steering | the speed curve, its smoothing, the lock | the game's own |
| brakes | the torque, the axles' shares, the game's ramp | the game's own full pedal on that car (the lower of its torque and its tyres), 0.92 of it at Heavy, both axles inside their tyres at their worst moment as the body dives (at most 0.95 then and 0.93 once settled), never so hard that the rear keeps less than 40% of its load; the game's own ABS module put on the car; the brake key eased from nothing to full in 0.15, 0.25 or 0.4 s |

So every turn the game's own car takes with the key held, up to its own top speed while it still holds the road, the car takes at the same radius (at most 3% wider: the little roll added moves a little weight out), its inner wheels keeping at least a tenth of their load, and the 9 m right turn, 15 m left turn and 30 m bend of the city's junctions and avenues at the speed the game's car takes them. Past that the tyres give a little more than the game's, and a tall van or truck slides before it would tip, which the game's own Mersaidi Dash, Lytte L6 and Vord Pony do not at their own top speed. The custom shop's parts go on top: a stiffer spring stays stiffer for its weight, the weight part's lighter car keeps its ride height and frequency, and on the grippiest tyres the car still slides before it rolls over.

Where the game's own car would roll over before its tyres let go (most of the softly sprung cars, the vans and the trucks), the tyres give sooner, past the game's hardest holding turn; where even that is not enough (the Mersaidi Dash, the Lytte L6 and the Vord Pony, and a little the UMC Desert, the Vord Tiara Vic and the Vord V150) the force points go up and the car leans a little less than the game's own. Where the springs are very stiff (the Ferdinand 112, the Mersaidi S500, the Vord Pony) the force points would have to go below the road for much more roll, so they roll only a little more than the game's. A heavier level rolls at least as much as a lighter one, except where rolling over holds it back (the fast sports cars, whose own turns reach 3.3 g).

**Cars of other mods.** The handling reads any car's own values when the player gets in, so a car of another mod always had it, but it kept the car's own ride, and the cars players add ride on springs the game never makes. A player's Player.log (November 2026) has a Ferrari SF90 Spider at 3.98 Hz, a BMW M4 G82 at 4.65 and an Audi R8 at 4.03 (springs of 237 000 to 279 000 N/m), against 0.76 to 2.88 Hz for the game's cars, so the body barely moved (0.12 to 0.2 degrees of roll per g, where the game's soft cars lean 4) and a 10 cm kerb bottomed their springs out: they stand on about 6 cm of travel at rest (the log says a 10 cm kerb leaves -4 cm), the game's soft cars on 16 to 21. Now a car whose type is not the game's (`CarCatalogue.IsGameCar`: a name that does not begin `ba:vehicletype_`; `HandlingRules.SoftensCar` takes the decision, with the option on) and rides more than 10% stiffer than the softest ride the game gives a car of its kind is softened in the first steps of its design (at Full and Heavy, Light leaves it as its mod made it). The ride asked (`HandlingRules.RideCeiling`) is 1.7 Hz for a sports car (the Anselmo AF90's 1.71) and 0.8 Hz for every other kind (the Vord V150's 0.76, the limousine's 0.81). Each axle's spring is made softer on its own curve and longer (`HandlingModel.Soften`), so that the same travel is left to compress at rest and the car stands as high, and its force points keep their height above the road. A softer spring on the same short travel cannot carry the same dive and roll, though: the first version asked 0.8 Hz of every car that is not a sports car whatever its travel, and the Audi R8 as a saloon, so softened, had its front 1.2 cm past its stops at the full pedal on the level's dampers the nose dropping until the body met the road. So the design tries rides (`HandlingModel.RideCarries`, eight at most, each in one or two design steps: its dive, then, where the dive is carried, its roll): the ride asked first, and if the car's travel at rest does not carry it the range between it and the car's own ride is halved seven times, and the softest ride that is carried is put on the car. A ride is carried when, on the design's own sums (the sliders and the custom shop's parts that are on the car counted), the front keeps a third of its travel at rest (`HandlingModel.FrontKeep`) at the peak of the full pedal's dive at the game's own angle (`HandlingModel.DiveCarries`; the Audi R8 as a saloon at 0.8 Hz kept -1.2 cm), and each axle's outer spring keeps a third of its travel in the hardest turn the car can take, the body swinging past its steady lean as a sudden turn swings it at the level's damping (`HandlingModel.RollCarries`, `HandlingModel.RollKept`). The lean counted there is the most a design of those springs can come to: the force points as low as the roll asked brings them (a design only raises them after, which leans the car less), the tyres holding what the game's own curve holds with no give past a knee (the grip slider and the custom shop's tyres counted) and never more than rolling over allows. The roll was first counted at 1 g of cornering, where the log's three cars hold about 3.6 g on their tyres as sports cars: the Audi R8 of the log as a saloon was given 1.005 Hz, which carried its dive and 1 g of its roll (1.47 degrees per g), but in the hardest turn its tyres hold its outer springs would go past their stops. A car whose own ride does not carry it either is left as it came. On 6 cm of travel the roll in the hardest turn can be what holds the ride (a centre of mass a little higher, lighter bars); at Heavy, whose lighter dampers swing the body further, that ride can be a little stiffer than at Full, and such a car may then lean a little less per g at Heavy than at Full, its outer springs keeping the same third of their travel in the hardest turn. EconomyCheck `--vehicle-feel` prints each car's ride and what its springs keep with `RLO_FEEL_REPORT=1`. Everything the design takes from the game's own car (the roll, the dive, the city's turns, the damping) is then taken from this softer car: the dampers are set for a damping ratio of 0.55 on the softer spring, and the roll and the dive come out as the game's cars'. The travel left to compress at rest is kept, so a kerb leaves what it left (the log's cars: -4 cm before and after), which no spring can lengthen, so the soft ride shows in the roll, the dive and the swing after a bump, not in a kerb. The option "Soft ride for other mods' cars" (`car_handling_modded`, under Car handling, on by default) switches it; with it off, at Light, and on a car of the game's own nothing changes (Empire Motors' two cars are built on the game's sports car, ride at about 1.7 Hz and are left as they are). It is for the car the player drives and owns (Going Public), made once with the design, nothing new each frame; the self-check line says what the springs rode at, what they ride at, where the travel at rest carried no more, and what the outer springs keep in the hardest turn its tyres hold, and FAILs a softened car that keeps less than a third there. A car the handling cannot read at all (not two axles of two wheels, wheels that are not the game's, a tyre or steering curve of weighted keys, no fuel tank, a motorbike) keeps its mod's own handling, and the test button and Player.log say why ("Handling: ... keeps the game's own handling"). The prefabs of these cars ship in their mods, so the checks run the model on cars built from the log's own figures (EconomyCheck `--vehicle-feel`, `VehicleFeelChecks.cs`: the three cars as the log has them, as sports cars and as every other kind, and a sweep of kinds, weights, centres of mass, travel and brakes; NavCheck's HandlingCheck designs them and four assumed stiff cars of other kinds with the ride asked and holds them to the envelope of the game's own cars, at Full and Heavy).

**The front springs' travel at the full pedal.** A full pedal's nose dive is not only its settled angle: the handling's lighter dampers let the body's first swing go about 40% further (the game's two-rate dampers act at under half their slow rate at the speeds of a dive), and nothing counted what that left of the front springs' travel. The model of the Vord V150 (its figures from a beta log: dive 2.37 degrees per g, springs of 9 747 N/m, damping ratio 0.55, 1.73 g at the full pedal) puts its front springs at 1.4 cm of 16.5 cm at the peak of a stop on the stock brakes (the game's own car, overdamped, keeps 8.6) and 5 cm past their stops with the custom shop's race brakes and semi-slick tyres (2.47 g): at the speeds the car now reaches (197 km/h), a stop of three or four seconds with the nose already down met every bump, joint and dip on its stops, and the car jolted. Now the front keeps at least a third of its travel at rest at the peak of the full pedal's dive (`HandlingModel.FrontKeep`), the front corner stepped on the handling's springs and dampers for 1.5 s as the brakes come on, the weight the dive presses on it growing with the brake as the model's ramp has it (`TravelLeft`), by three means, in this order. (1) The nose dive the level asks for is held back (`HandlingModel.DiveForTravel`), towards the game's own and never below it, and kept to what the anti-squat can give: its limits (plus and minus one) set the dives a car can have, so the travel is read at the dive the car comes to (it was read at the dive planned before the limit, and for the Mersaidi Dash with sliders moved, the tall van and the heavy truck of NavCheck the figure in the line was not the car's). (2) Where the game's own dive still leaves less, the front dampers' compression rate is firmed (`HandlingModel.FirmFront`), from what the level set towards `MostDamping` (twice) the game's own rate for the weight, until the front keeps what it is held to (below) or that limit is reached. Only the compression: the rebound, which the bounce after a kerb is measured on, stays as the level set it. What was left after the dive was held to the game's own was this swing: the Vord V150 at Full kept 4.05 cm of 16.5 (Heavy 4.70), under the third (5.46) the hold-back itself asks, and with its compression firmed 1.4 times (1.2 at Heavy) keeps 5.46 cm. (3) The full pedal is planned for the custom shop's brakes, weight and tyres that are on the car when the player gets in (`CarChassis.PartsBrakes`, `PartsMass` and `PartsGrip`, read by `CarHandling.Begin` against the car with the parts lifted off), and again whenever the Garage lays other brakes, weight or tyres on the car being driven or takes them off (`GarageBridges.PartsLaid`, `HandlingRules.Relay`: brakes bought sitting in the car, or the road turning dry under semi-slick tyres, came on top of a pedal held for the parts that were there before, unheld), the design made again beside the one on the car and put on in its place in one frame, and what the parts add to the car's own full pedal is held where the front springs would keep less than the handling holds them to (`HandlingModel.HeldForTravel`, in `PartsBrakes`, so the shop's stopping figure says it too). The pickup with the sport brakes (1.25 times the torque) brakes at 1.93 g at Full where the parts ask 2.17, with the race brakes (1.5 times) at 2.01 g (2.60 asked), with the race brakes and semi-slick tyres at 2.40: the front keeps its 5.46 cm in each, where it kept 0.7, -0.2 and -3.4 cm once the shop's parts were on the car.

What the front is held to (`HandlingModel.TravelNeeded`) is a third of its travel at rest or, where the game's own car keeps less (`HandlingSetup.GameFrontLeft`: the same corner on the game's springs, dampers and dive, the same brake ramp), that. The Lytte L6 (7.7 cm of travel; the game's own car keeps 1.3 cm of it) keeps 1.3 cm at Full with its compression firmed 1.8 times (1.4 at Heavy, not at all at Light), and the Mersaidi Dash (3.6 cm) -1.5 cm where the game's own car keeps -1.5 (firmed 2.2 times): cars with so little travel go onto their stops in the game's own, and the handling puts them there no sooner, nor further. The cars with travel to spare (the sports cars, the Nunavut, the trucks) dive as before and keep the dampers the level set; the Honza Mimic a little less (3.1 degrees per g at Full where the level asks 3.3 and the game's own is 2.8) and the Vord Tiara Vic as the game's own car dives (NavCheck prints every figure with `--handling`). The braking power at the car's own brakes is the tyres' limit with the body's dive in it, so a dive held back to the game's lets a short rear spring carry more, never more than the game's own times the level's share: the Lytte L6 brakes at 2.28 g at Full (2.14 g before the dive was held back, the game's own 2.39). Only what the custom shop's parts add is held to the travel. The self-check line says what the front keeps with the parts on the car, the game's own car's figure, whether the extra dive was held back and by how much the dampers were firmed, and FAILs when it keeps less than it is held to.

**The custom shop's parts on top.** The Garage's parts act on the handling as they act on the game's own car: the springs part makes the spring its factor stiffer on its own curve and stands the car the part's height lower or higher at rest (`HandlingModel.FitSpring`), never into the 12 cm of travel a kerb needs; the weight part carries a lighter car at the same height and frequency; the dampers follow the square root of both factors, so the damping ratio stays; the force points move with the body; the bars take the factor; the brakes part the torque, only as far as the tyres take it (the axles' shares are set again for the parts on top); the tyres part the grip. NavCheck runs every car at every level with every build of springs, weight and tyres, wet and dry: still well damped, a stiffer spring stiffer for its weight, and sliding before rolling over even on the grippiest tyres. The custom shop's stopping figure is the handling's own model with the build on top (`HandlingModel.ShopStop`); NavCheck steps the car braking wheel by wheel, each corner's dive counted, for every car and level with every build of brakes, weight, tyres and springs at both of the game's onsets: the two agree within a metre, and no wheel asks more than its tyre holds.

**Every value comes back.** Everything is recorded first (the mass and inertia, every wheel's spring force and length, dampers' rates, force point, anti-squat, load rating, rolling resistance and tyres, each axle's bar and brake share, the two steering curves, the ground preset, the ABS module) and put back exactly when the player gets out or changes car, when the option or a slider changes, on a fault, when the driving feature is cleaned up and when the city goes (`CarTuning.Restore`), with the Garage's parts lifted off around it and laid back on top. The springs' curves, the dampers' shape, the tyres' slip stiffness and the centre of mass are never changed. The power, gearing and drag are worked out after the handling is on (for the new weight) and taken off before it.

**Testing.** With Developer diagnostics on, getting in writes one line to Player.log, "Handling check (getting in): ...": the kind and level; the weight, centre of mass, springs, ride, damping ratio, a kerb's travel, roll, force points, bars, dive, rolling over, what the tyres hold and where they give, the hardest turn they were measured at, steering and brakes against the game's own; then PASS, or what did not take. The line also says what a softened car rode at before (and that the travel at rest carried no softer ride, where it did not), what the front springs keep of their travel at the peak of the full pedal's dive with the custom shop's parts on the car against the game's own car (whether the extra dive was held back and the front dampers' compression firmed for it), and ends FAIL, with the figures, when they keep less than they are held to. What the handling wrote is read back before the Garage's parts go on top of it, so a springs part on the car is never reported as a fault (0.40.4 said "the springs or tyres did not take" whenever the custom shop's parts re-fitted the springs on top). Getting out writes "Handling check (getting out): ...: PASS" once every value reads back as the game had it. The test button "Say what the car you drive has from its handling" gives a short notice, and the full line in Player.log whatever the diagnostics say. With the test tools shown, the options page's Troubleshooting has "Car handling tuning": six sliders from 50 to 150% (spring softness, bounce, body roll, tyre grip, steering at speed, how quickly the brakes build), around the level's figures as before: 100% is now the game's own springs, damping, grip and steering with the level's additions on top. A saved level and saved sliders keep working as they are.

Not seen in the game yet: how the turns, the little roll and the give past the limit feel against the model; that the game's ABS module, put on while the car runs, eases the brakes as on a car built with it; a car on the two terrains; Empire Motors' cars and other mods' cars, whose values the checks can only assume.

## Turn signals

**What the player sees.** The car the player drives signals its turns by itself. Once the car has been turning for 0.4 s, the signal of the side it turns to comes on, through the game's own signal (the one the two signal keys toggle, with its tick), and it goes off when the turn is made. The two keys work as ever. A signal the player switched on is theirs: the mod never puts it off and never adds the other side beside it. The option is "Automatic turn signals", on by default, in Options, Getting around, At the wheel; with it off the game behaves exactly as without it.

**The rules** (`Driving/TurnSignalRules`, game-free; `TurnSignals` looks at the driven car ten times a second and hands the rules its speed along the nose, the steering asked for, how fast the nose is swinging and which signals show):

| what | figure | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a turn | the car moving forward at 9 km/h or more, the wheel at least half over and the nose swinging 9 degrees a second or more | parking at a crawl, backing and a slide with the wheel straight are not turns |
| the signal comes on | the turn has lasted 0.4 s, no signal shows, and a second has passed since the last one went off | the taps on a key that keep a car in its lane are shorter, and one signal rests before the next |
| the signal stays | a stop in the middle of a turn keeps it, however long | a car held at a junction is still turning |
| the signal goes off | the nose has swung 20 degrees the signalled way, the wheel and the nose have been straight for 0.8 s and the signal has been up 4 s (two full flashes of the game's own, whose cycle is 2 s) | the turn is made |
| a signal that came on for nothing | goes off after 6 s of straight running at a pace | the turn was never made |
| backing | takes it off at once, at 1.5 m/s backwards or more | the car is no longer going the way it was signalling |
| the other way | a turn the other way for 0.4 s, after the signal has stood 2 s, puts it over to the other side (the game's toggle puts the first off) | an S-bend; a weave does not make it flicker |
| the driver's own | a signal that shows and was not switched on by the rules is never touched, and none is raised beside it; one the driver puts off in a turn is not raised again until the car has run straight for 0.5 s | the keys stay the driver's |

**Who it is for.** Only the car the player drives, and never another player's car in a Going Public session (`GoingPublic.MineVehicle`). Traffic signals its own turns through the game's driving (`DrivingAI` and the cars' `VehicleLightsComponent`). Of the mod's own drivers only the city bus indicates, pulling in to its bay and away (`BusVehicle.Indicate`); the taxi, the response units and the reckless drivers, all driven by `EmergencyDriver`, show none, and this feature does not change that.

**Gives back.** What the rules switched on is switched off again whenever the car is let go: the option switched off, another car driven, the player getting out, a load, a fault, the feature switched off after faults and the city going. A car the game gave no signal object (a car of another mod) keeps its own signals, and so does a car whose signal, once switched on, does not show where the game keeps it: the switch is put back at once, with a warning in Player.log. Whenever the signals cannot work on a car (no vehicle physics of the game's kind, no signal object, no body mesh to read the signals from), Player.log says why, once for the car.

**Cost.** Idle, one comparison with the clock a frame. With a car followed, a look ten times a second: a few field reads, the nose's heading and one read of the body's property block, nothing allocated.

**Checks.** EconomyCheck, `--turn-signals` (`TurnSignalChecks.cs`): the rules through every kind of drive on a car that has the game's two signals (a left and a right turn, taps in a lane, parking pace, backing, a gentle bend, a slide, a roundabout, an S-bend, a weave, a turn never made, a stop in a turn, a late look), the driver's own signals (left alone, put off in a turn, put over to the other side, switched on as the turn begins), the cost (a million looks allocate nothing), the sources (the option, the guard, the player's own car only, every way the car is let go, the game's signal object named in one file) and the texts. With Developer diagnostics on, Player.log has `Turn signal check (...)` lines: one for each signal put on (the nose's swing and the speed against what the rules want, and whether the game's signal took it), one for each put off and one for each car given back, each ending PASS or the reason.

Not seen in the game yet: that the signal flashes and ticks on every car as the key's does (the game's own routine drives both), how the thresholds feel with a keyboard and with a pad, and the cars of other mods.
