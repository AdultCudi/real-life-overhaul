# Flare in the city

## In a sentence

Under the living economy's master option **Living population** and its own option **Flare in the city**, Flare's followers are the population's residents: every adult follows a few of the city's accounts from who they are, a post is seen resident by resident, and what residents hear of a business draws them to it from its rivals in the same pools (never made from nothing), measured the next day in visits and takings; every business has a page with its followers, a rating and reviews by residents who really shopped there, ads reach the residents they target, and the city's people on the feed are residents too. The city's businesses pay the player for posts as the account grows, Flare pays a creator income from Influencer on, a game studio of the player's posts its news and asks the residents who play games for hype and pledges, a viral thread moves the market by the investors it reached, the player's word in an election is worth the city's adults who follow them, what goes viral starts trends that move the residents' tastes for a few weeks, and once a week the player, or an office's manager, plans the week on Flare at a desk.

## The follows

A resident aged 14 or over follows up to eight accounts, made from who they are the first day Flare meets them: for each kind of spending their household cares for, now and then a shop of that kind in their district (by its room for customers), their grocer, a voice or two of their district, the influencers of their interests (a food influencer only by residents who care for eating out), and on the first day the player, at the account's city share. The player's city share is the account's followers times its tier's share (90% for a newcomer to 25% for a celebrity, at most 85% of the city's adults); the rest are "beyond the city", an aggregate as the population's region is. The account's own rules (`FlareRules.Post`, silence, a bad post, an arrest) still decide the follower count; each day the city's followers are brought to it, residents of the player's districts first when it grows, the lowest rolls of the day going when it falls. A post's new followers in the city are residents who saw it, were interested and did not follow. A business's followers come from its customers (one for every 400 visits, drawn from the pools) and drift away slowly (3 per mille a day), so a shop's following settles near its daily visits. Somebody who dies or leaves takes their follows with them; somebody new in their slot starts afresh.

## A post heard

Each follower sees an ordinary post at Flare's own reach share (30% times its quality and tone fit; 75% viral). Those who care for what it is about are interested 65% of the time, others 22%. An interested resident shares it 5% of the time (20% viral) with their household, two people at work and a neighbour, each seeing it 60% of the time. A few residents of the business's district come across it by its tags (0.3% of them; 4% viral). Each resident counts once a post. An interested resident who hears of a business adds one to the district's count of who heard good (or bad) things, as the share of the district who had not heard yet, so a district's count never passes its adults.

What is heard: the player's posts and replies, reviews and announcements (the player's, the voices', the session players'), the city's businesses' own posts, influencers' visits, a hire that backfired or an influencer's scandal, a complaint's inspection, a guest's post about a business on the host. Paid influencer deals and the Company app's sponsored post stay the Company's campaigns (their customers through the population's levers), so nothing is counted twice.

## Attention, people moved and the lift

A business's attention is the share of its customers' residents who heard good things times 1.5, less the share who heard bad things times 0.9 (thousandths), each district weighed by the money its residents brought the business's district for its kind on the day before. It is one of the population's attention sources (`PopulationAttention`, registered in `Wiring.FlareCityLinks`), asked once a game day: the pools are only ever shared out, so attention moves people from rivals, and a district's pull counts its businesses' attention, so a talked-of street draws people from other districts too. What residents heard fades by a quarter a day (bad talk 12% a day until answered, half a day after); the pools' habit keeps the customers it won a while longer.

The next morning Flare measures the lift of each business: its share of each class's pool with the attention it had, against the same pools with Flare's part taken out (the pools' money routed again without it, the business's own share recovered from its weight and the habit share it came in with, taken at the day's first ask). EconomyCheck runs twin cities and finds it within 5% of the real difference. The lift is shared among the player's posts and ads by their live impressions' share of what the residents heard of the business (the rest is the city's own talk); a lift that kept people away is no post's.

## Reviews, pages, ads and the voices

A customer is drawn from the day's pools as its money came (a class by the business's takings by class, a district by the money its residents routed there, a household by its day's budget for the kind), so the reviewer's household really spent there that day (checked against `PoolRules.Attribute`). One review for every 300 visits; the stars follow the business's satisfaction, a little for its interior, less where its prices are past what the reviewer's class bears. A review is read by the reviewer's household and two at work, and by residents of the district who care for its kind; four stars or more is good talk, two or fewer bad. Each business keeps its newest six reviews and a fading rating.

An ad (Business, Run an ad) is shown each day to so many residents of its target (its district or the city, those who care for what it sells, the young, the well off) in a rolled order; it costs 22 a thousand residents a day at today's prices, booked at the business (`reallife_transaction_flare_ad`), shown on its first day at once and measured from the next like a post. The city's own businesses and the rivals run ads (six a day at normal busyness), post and are reviewed by the same rules.

The four hundred people of the feed ("c0" to "c399") are residents drawn from the city's adults, the young and students more likely; their age, sex, district and manner (a student, a pensioner, a parent of a child at home, or by their household's tastes) are their resident's, and their name is rolled from the city's seed, the slot and the resident (`FlareLiveRules.ResidentCitizen`). A voice whose resident died or left is somebody new.

## The player's name

Each day 2.5% of the player's followers in the city hear of each of the player's businesses because they follow its founder, so a big following lifts them all; while an arrest that trended this week has no honest post answering it, 40% of them hear of it against each business.

## Paid posts and a creator's income

From Local on, the city's businesses (the city's own and the rivals', never a business of the player's or of a session player) offer to pay for a post about them: a roll a day by the account's tier (4% at Local to 25% at Celebrity), at most one offer open at a time, the business drawn by its room for customers. A post pays 12 for each thousand followers at today's prices, times the city's trust in the account (its reputation, 0.75 to 1.25), 25 at the least. From Known on a quarter of the offers are sponsorships: four posts, one every three days, each at 90% of a single post's fee. An offer stands three days on the Business tab; taking it puts the first post up at once (a paid partnership, `reallife_flare_post_deal_*`) and pays it; the rest go up on their days by themselves. A paid post is the player's post about the business: heard by the player's followers like any post, so the business that paid gets real customers from it, measured in the post's funnel. The Company's own campaigns (influencer deals, the sponsored post) stay the Company's, with their customers through the population's levers.

From Influencer on, Flare pays a creator income every 30 days: 3 for each thousand views the player's posts had in those days, at today's prices. The first month counts from the day the account reached Influencer.

## The game studio on Flare

The game studio (Tech/Studio) asks Flare through `StudioBridges` (`Talk`, `Devlog`, `Crowdfund`), set by `Wiring.FlareCityLinks`. A studio's news (the announcement, a trailer, the expo, previews, the launch, the critics' score, an update, an add-on, an award) is a post on the studio's own account ("s:" and its place), heard by its followers and by residents of its district who come across it, those who play games interested (the population's games); while the residents' day is being worked it is heard once it is done. A devlog is the studio's post shared by the player (heard by the player's followers at 70% of a post): its hype is 0.4 thousandths for each interested resident over the city's adults who play games, 8% of the audience at the most (a trailer's). A crowdfunding campaign is backed by the followers of the player or the studio who play games and see it (30%), 12% of them, each by their household's class (10, 25 or 60), and by 8 per mille of the player's followers beyond the city at 20 each, never past the goal; each resident counts once. With the residents' side off, on a guest, or while its day is being worked, the player's followers stand in (a devlog's hype a few points for each tenfold, a campaign's pledges as those beyond the city give).

The other tech firms (Tech/Sector) tell Flare their news through `SectorBridges.Talk` (the kind, `SectorNews`; the firm's place, null for a rival's; a name; a figure), set by `Wiring.FlareCityLinks`. A firm of the player's posts on its own business page by the same rules as the studio: a launch, a benchmark or an award, an outage, a breach or an incident (`reallife_flare_sector_news_*`), heard by its followers and by the residents of its district who care for digital things (the population's Digital kind); where the population counts the firm, good news draws residents and bad keeps them away a while. A rival's news (a product shipped, a price cut, a tender won over a firm of the player's) is the city's own post by the rival business, made on the host alone in a Going Public session, as the rest of the city's feed; a guest has the host's. Each post is seeded from the save; a studio's or a firm's news is heard as it is posted and never again with the city's own posts of the day.

## The market and the council

A viral post about a business is heard by the residents first; the city's retail investors among those interested (residents whose household has 10,000 or more in savings) give its trade's sector one buzz on the stock market and one more for each hundred of them, eight at the most. The market-abuse rule stands as it was. A post about an issue moves the feed's mood about the city's running by the residents it reached: Flare's own 10 points when 2% of the city's adults were interested, between 2 and 30. The player's word in an election (`CivicBridges.Following`) is worth the share of the city's adults who follow the player, all of it when half of them do, for a trusted account (reputation 50 or more; less in step below).

## Trends

What goes viral moves the residents' tastes (`FlareCityRules.Trends`). A viral post of the player's about a place to go out (night life, eating out, coffee, the theatre, the cinema, a casino) makes its district's going out of that kind a trend; one about a shop of goods with a taste behind it (clothes, gadgets, furniture, books and gifts, flowers, jewellery, the gym, hair and beauty, coffee, eating out) makes that kind a trend in the whole city; a grocer starts none. The city's own talk starts one about once a month (3% a day): half the time a kind of goods, a quarter a district's going out (the district by its adults), a quarter a game genre. At most three run at once, and never the same twice at a time.

A trend rises for 5 days: each day more of its own take to it, everyone whose roll falls under the share the day has reached and who has not the taste yet. Goods: by its height 25% of the city's households without the taste have it, so they spend more of their money on the kind (the population's `SpendRules.Shares` tilts a liked kind by 30%, the total kept): about 5% more spent on it in the city. A district's going out: 30% of the district's households take to it, and each day each district's adults hear of its places of the kind (4% a day of those who had not, at its height), so their attention draws people there from the same pools elsewhere, about a fifth more pull at its height. A genre: 6% of the adults who play games and did not like it take to it (the population's genre bits), which the game studio's audience counts, about a quarter more for that genre. It holds until day 21, then fades over 5 days, letting a fifth of those it moved go each day, all but the 10% who keep the taste for good. A post about what is trending interests 15% more of the residents who hear it. The households (or people) a trend moved are kept by slot and id, so after a load they are found again by id as it fades.

Explore lists the trends at the top (each with how far along it is, how many it moved and the days left), a guest's from the host's part; a notice says when one begins, and when the player's viral post began it.

## Crises and boycotts

A post of the player's that went badly and was seen widely (Flare's pile-on) is a scandal for the residents as an arrest that trended is: for 7 days, unless an honest post of the player's answers it, 40% of the player's followers in the city hear of it against each of the player's businesses. A scandal a brand had at a shop (Brands) is heard by the shop's district as bad talk, so its residents stay away from it until the talk fades or is answered; an influencer's scandal and a hire that backfired are heard by their followers the same way.

## The desk session

Once a week the week on Flare is planned at the owner's desk in one of the player's offices (the tech firms' and the game's two office businesses, as the weekly team meeting; Scripts/Sessions), or by that office's manager when it is handed to one: a session of the sessions' registry (`flare_content_plan`, `Wiring.FlareSessionLinks`), listed while Flare in the city runs. Its three steps: this week's posts (a post a day, seven; three strong posts, one every other day; none), poor reviews (answer them all, so the bad talk at each of the player's businesses fades faster; leave them), and if a storm breaks (answer honestly; stay quiet). Each planned post is the player's own announcement about the next of their businesses, made from 10:00 on its day by Flare's own rules (its reach and followers, the repeat that makes each of a run worth a little less, the residents who hear it). Answering honestly answers a storm standing now, and one that breaks within the week the day it breaks: an honest post about an arrest, or an honest word of the player's after a pile-on, which ends the scandal against their businesses. A manager makes the session's choices by Flare's sound ones (a post a day, every review answered, honesty), and makes each planned post and answer at their strength (half at no skill, all at 100). One plan a week for the whole account: a session at another office waits until the week is out.

## The screens

`FlareCityViews` holds each screen as data for the interface kit (living/ui): the account in the city (followers here and beyond, the newest posts' and ads' funnels), a business's page (followers, rating, pull, the newest reviews by their residents, what it heard and brought, one action: Run an ad), an ad (target, reach, days, price, or why it cannot run), one post's funnel and the offers (each business, its fee a post, its posts, where it stands; the open one can be taken). Until the kit draws them, Flare's app shows the same data: a business card's lines and its Run an ad button, the ad sheet, the offers on the Business tab with Take the offer, and the account's lines at the top of the player's own Profile.

## The save and Going Public

One line, `reallife:flare_city`: binary columns compressed in pieces on the save's workers (the district save's zip), read on a worker at a load; the follows by person id and the businesses' figures by key, so they find their person and their business wherever the population's slots are; then the offers of paid posts, the creator income's month and a pile-on's day, the trends with the households and people they moved (by id, as steps from the one before), and the week's content plan. A save without it makes the follows on the first day; a line it cannot read is left until the next save. The line's version stays 1: every part of it came on this branch, never released.

In a session the host owns the residents and what they hear, as it owns the population: its guests' posts about a business are heard by its residents, and their businesses' customers come from its pools. Part Q (`FlareCityShare`, "FR1", a letter older versions do not know and leave out) carries the host's voices, so the city's people have the same names and faces on every machine, each session player's businesses' figures, which a guest's cards show, and the host's trends ("T" lines), which a guest's Explore shows. A guest runs no residents of its own, cannot run ads, gets no offers and holds no desk session; a rival tech firm's news is posted by the host alone, as the rest of the city's feed, and a guest's own studio's and firms' news are its own business posts, as before.
