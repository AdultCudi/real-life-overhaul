## In a sentence

Buildings going up in the city as the game itself would show them: a whole building wrapped to its roof in the game's own scaffolding and fabric while it is rebuilt, and on ground given to it a new building's hoarding, dig, slab, tower crane, frame storeys, cladding and wrap, stage by stage over game days.

## What players see

**A rebuild** (one at a time in the city, about 160 working hours, drawn by the programme like any site; never the player's own building, never one of the mod's own places, none a DLC keeps):

1. Hoarding along the door's wall, the notice board, two people at it.
2. The game's scaffold going up every street side, half its lifts, no fabric; one of the crew on the first deck.
3. Wrapped to the roof, the fabric hung from the second lift up; people on the deck at work and a painter; the skip at the kerb.
4. The inspection: an inspector with a clipboard at the notice board, one person on the deck.
5. The scaffold coming down to half, no fabric; then the site is handed over and everything goes once nobody is looking.

At the handover the building wears a new look of the same size, a design of the city's own stood over it by the copy engine (`BuildingCopies.PlaceOver`, its own look hidden), out of view; the works state keeps the look (`SiteLook`, at most 96) and it stands again behind the loading screen at every load (`SiteLooks`).

**A new plot** (on ground given to it; about 320 working hours):

1. Hoarding along the front with a 6 m gate at the yard, the fence wire round the rest, the site cabin and a toilet in the yard.
2. The dig: the frame's footprint broken up, spoil heaped round it; two people digging.
3. The ground slab with its starter bars, the tower crane on its pad in the yard with its jib slewing, material stacks.
4. The frame rising a storey at a time (3.6 m a storey, columns on a grid of spans up to 6 m, a slab each, edge protection round the top).
5. Every storey but the top clad in metal panels, and the frame wrapped in the game's scaffolding, fabric on.

At the handover the frame comes down out of view and the finished building stands on the plot, a design of the city's own fitted to the frame's footprint (`BuildingCopies.PlaceOnPlot`), kept and stood again at every load as a rebuild's is, until the lot holds a building of the district's own. Whatever of a plot stands between the camera and the player is hidden the way the game hides its own buildings (their shadows kept).

**A narrow plot** (7 to 20 m of land along the street, 20 m deep at least: 7 m is one space of the district, the standard lowest lot) keeps a metre each side and its yard behind the frame, goes up to six storeys without a tower crane, and has its gate at the front's end. Plots on the lots of a district may stand side by side: a plot refuses only a site on its own ground and 2 m round it.

## The game's scaffolding

- **What it is:** the Buildings scene's `SM_Scaffolding_8m` (6.0 m), `_4m` and `_Corner_4m` (3.0 m) modules, 3.04 m deep, and `SM_Scaffolding_LargeFabric` and `_SmallFabric`, stacked at 5.17 m on 26 of the game's buildings.
- **Layout:** one run along each street side (pavement under the whole depth, no other building in it), a corner module at each end (the far one turned a quarter, as the game's), 6 m modules between and one 3 m where the count is odd, slid along the slack until no post stands within 0.95 m of a door's middle, else split round the door. A tree, a sign or a lamp over a stretch splits the run; a bin or hydrant where a post would stand keeps the posts off it. Lifts: the top deck within half a lift of 0.6 m under the roof, at most ten, at most 360 pieces.
- **Fade:** the pieces' renderers join the building's `renderersToFade` and `renderersToHide` and its private set the city map swaps for its box (`_lodRenderers`), and leave them before a piece is destroyed; the pieces hang under a holder whose `ViewBlockingEntityExtension` names the building (set before the holder wakes), so the camera's ray meeting the scaffold fades the building with it, as the game's own scaffold does. Two lines of `Core/GameContract.cs` (part `site_wrap_fade`).
- **Pace:** nothing is set out or taken down while the wrap's box is in the camera's view (the test buttons excepted), never while the city map shows, six pieces a frame (twelve to take down, sixty behind the game's black screen).
- **Pieces:** copies of a building's own scaffold made once a city; where every building's is static-batched they are made from the game's meshes and materials found by name instead.

## Props and clips

The building sites' props are in a second props bundle, `Bundles/reallife_props_site` (PropAssets/README.md, "The second bundle"): `tower_crane_mast`, `tower_crane_jib` (turning about its pivot, the slewing ring, by Unity's Animation), `site_cabin`, `site_toilet`, `site_stack_blocks`, `site_stack_timber`, `site_stack_rebar`, `site_earthworks` and the `hammer`. ## Going Public

The new kinds are values of the site's existing kind field, and a plot's ground is text in its existing street field: the save's line and the host's line of sites keep their shape and version, and nothing new is sent. The host draws the programme as before (seeded, the same on every machine); a guest follows it. The wrap and the plot's meshes are drawn by each machine from the same site records. The test buttons that change sites are the host's.

### What players see: the loop

1. **The company.** An office opened as a <b>Construction Company</b> in BizMan (an office business type of the mod's, its staff the game's own designers and client staff at the office's desks). In the **Construction** app: Found the company.
2. **The licence.** The contractor's licence at City Hall: a fee, a decision two days on, refused while a conviction counts; a year, renewed in its last days.
3. **Land.** The Land tab lists the new district's free spaces for sale (the owner, 25 Oct 2026: a lot is the whole block, with many buildings in it): one row for each run of free spaces side by side along a block's side (`ConstructionDirector.Runs`), its address, its spaces and frontage, and a space's price, 68% of the value of the building a lot of its class usually holds, at the district's price a square metre, its type's multiplier and the space's own traffic, as the game values a building (`Building.GetMarketPricePerSqm`). Its page's selector picks how many of its spaces, from its first, to buy as one site (**Buy the spaces**, `BuySite`): one site, one building, its land kept as its first space's (`LotFile.Spaces`), its other spaces held with it so no one else buys them. Several sites can stand in one block, beside its buildings from the first day. Each site is zoned for some uses by its spaces (one a small building's, two an office's, three or more a warehouse's); the city's council can add one (asked at City Hall, heard at its Thursday planning sitting or at the player's own meeting), or allow a use on every lot of a class. Land tax each week, more on land kept empty. Or a building of the player's own: **Plan a rebuild** (it keeps its own spaces: a design of its own size, on its own footprint).
4. **Plans.** The use (one the lot is zoned for), a design of the city's own that fills the site (the copy engine's measured designs: of a size taking exactly the site's spaces, or fewer when its frontage alone fills it within a metre, `BuildCatalogue.Offered`, so no ground is left inside a site beside its building; the frame's footprint is the site's scaffolding), the architect (a practice for a fee, an architect of the staff, or the player once qualified), parking under the building or none. The plans write in the fire exits; the parking asked, the district's height limit (12 storeys in the new district, 8 under strict permits: the elected government's building code) and an engineer's sign off (from three storeys, and for every rebuild) are requirements. An estimator of the staff narrows the estimate's error.
5. **Planning permission** at City Hall (see below for why City Hall's call): the fee, the neighbours' objections in the City Ledger, the council's decision in six to fourteen working days, conditions with many objections, a refusal with its reasons; an appeal to the court (a lawyer of the player's own helps) or the plans drawn again.
6. **The site.** With the licence, permission and a crew: the site starts in the city's programme, a plot on the lot's ground or the game's scaffolding round the building. It works at the crews' pace (their trades against the stage's need, skill, morale, the plant, the speciality), as far as the stage's materials allow. A stage's end holds the works only for the inspection that ends it: with none (a plot's groundworks at 40%, a rebuild's at 20%) or once it has passed, they go straight on into the next stage (`PlansRules.WorkingStage`; a working hour's call is `SiteRules.Hour`, the director only acts on it). The programme says which hour it works (`SiteBuilders.Hours`, day x 24 + hour), and catches a sleep up at once on waking (it counts in the game's scaled time, which the game's time machine stops), so bookings, the inspector's visits and a handover are dated by the hour worked, never by the waking hour. A building whose site the programme no longer holds is set out again at the day's pass where its stage began (`SitesLost`); while the construction game has faulted its sites wait, as while it is off.
7. **Materials, plant, crews.** Each stage's loads ordered from one of three builders' merchants, priced off the raw materials exchange's building materials (their move against the city's price level, `Markets.MarketDirector.MaterialMove`), delivered at 07:00 on a working day, sometimes short; what a project will still buy can be hedged with futures (**Materials and hedge**). Plant hired a day, or bought and kept at a yard (a warehouse of the player's); a tower crane from three storeys. Crews hired from the week's labour market, paid each working day, their morale up and down, strikes, walking off, a rival's offer to match.
8. **Inspections.** Three visits (after the foundations, after the frame, at the end), booked, the inspector from 10:00 on the next working day (two when booked from 15:00), in the site's own working hour or the director's hourly pass, whichever comes first (`SiteRules.Visits`); a failure means rework, done by the crews at the stage's end before the inspection falls due again; a bribe waves it through and may come out for 21 days (a case of bribery, the company's reputation and Standing). A site manager of the staff books, orders, gets the plant and puts a free crew on.
9. **Money.** A construction loan at the business loan's rate and 1.5 points, drawn in stages and on the credit file; an investor's money for a share more; a block's flats sold before they are built (a deposit now, the rest at completion). Theft, storms and faults, and insurance's share.
10. **The handover.** The site is handed over: the building's new look stands out of view (below), a plot's building is handed to the district (one of its buildings, the player's own or its residents': it stands as the scaffolding comes down, or at the next black screen if its ground is in view, [Uptown](uptown.md), "A building handed over stands at once"), the City Ledger has it, and the next day is its opening.
11. **The opening.** Between 10:00 and 18:00 of the opening day, at the building's front, the game's own panel offers **Cut the ribbon** (`GameUi.DoorPanel`): a moment on Flare, a story, a lift in the company's reputation and Standing. A notice comes the evening before (18:00) and on the morning (08:00), and the objectives show the opening as urgent from the handover (`OpeningRules`, `TrackRules`). Unattended, it opens by itself at 18:00 and says once what that cost: Standing 1 instead of 3, no moment on Flare, no story (`OpeningMissed`).
12. **Afterwards.** A plot's building is the player's once it stands (used, let or sold through BizMan: the district writes the game's own deed as it stands), or a block's flats are sold one by one at the asking price chosen, the building its residents'. A rebuild's building carries on with its new look.

**Without a company, or instead of the company's crews: hire a builder** ([below](#hiring-a-builder)). Land, plans and planning permission (steps 3 to 5) need no company; only the company's own crews need it and its licence.

Alongside: **Tenders** (the city's own works as its sites start, a business's rebuild every six to ten days, a public building on an office lot every fifteen to twenty-five), bid at 85% to 130% of the value, usual or fast, the best mix of price, time and reputation winning; the **Rivals** tab (the city's eight contractors, Civic's names, each known for a kind of work, with its office in Uptown); the **Company** tab (office, licence, reputation, speciality, insurance, yard, record, books).

**The rivals' offices.** Each of the eight rival builders has an office in Uptown, in one of the district's eight leased office lots (Uptown's `BuildingsRules.Leased`, lease `i` rival `i`'s; [Uptown](uptown.md), "The leased offices"). The wiring leases them (`Wiring.ConstructionLinks.RivalLease`, setting `DistrictBuildings.Lessee` whatever any option says, so every machine leases the same): a business of the construction company's type (`ConstructionType.TypeName`), set up as the game's graphic design studios are (`ConstructionType.Template`: their layouts, hours and sign), under the rival's name, with BizMan's description (`BuildCatalogue.OfficeAboutKey`) and valued by its crews (`BuildCatalogue.OfficeDayPerCrew`, $1,200 a crew a day). So the map's row for the Construction Company type outlines the eight, BizMan presents each as a business of the game's (held off the market: no rent, sale or takeover), and a player walking in finds a studio's furniture and staff. The **Rivals** tab gives each one's address (`ConstructionBridges.RivalOffice`) and its button makes it the game's destination (`ConstructionBridges.RivalOfficeGo`); with Uptown not standing at a load, a rival has no office then and the tab says so. Nothing of it is in the construction game's line: the offices' registrations are Uptown's, kept by the district save.

**Why City Hall's call for planning:** City Hall is already a contact with topics in the game's own conversation panel (Civic's `CityHallDialog`), the licence and zoning belong with it, and a call needs nothing in the street; a desk on a floor would need a building held for City Hall, which the floors core has not got. The app opens the same pages (`PlanningCall.Open`), on the project's application when it can be made.

### Hiring a builder

2. **The quotes** (`ConstructionApp.Hire.cs`, the project's third page): each of the eight builders bids as on a tender (`TenderRules.RivalBid` through `CommissionRules.Bids`, the loop the refit's contractor quotes through too), the works valued at the plans' estimate with a client's margin (`TenderRules.BuildingValue`), its days of works drawn round the usual days of the hours its site is really set out with in the city's working day (`CommissionRules.UsualDays`: the programme's hours a day at its speed under the building code, not a tender's ten), homes or offices the speciality for a plot and renovation for a rebuild, and a builder busy with the city's works asking more. Pick one, **The cheapest** or **The fastest**, then **Hire this builder** (the game's question box).
3. **The contract** (`BuildCatalogue.Hire*`): a deposit of 10% as it is signed (`ConstructionGame.Take`: refused without the cash), 25%, 30% and 30% as the foundations, frame and final inspections pass (`Force`, as a contract's payments run on), and the last 5% kept back to the handover. Late, the builder gives back 0.5% of the price a day, 10% at most, out of the retention and then in cash (`CommissionRules.Settle`). Every payment is the game's own transaction (`reallife_transaction_bld_builder`), counted in the project's spending and so in the building's price for the district.
4. **The works:** the site is set out at once in the city's programme by the company's own starter (`StartSite`: a plot's frame or a rebuild's scaffolding, the builder named on its notice board). The builder's site goes through the company's own working hour (`Work`): the same four stages and three inspections, each stage held until the inspector passes it, at the builder's pace (`CommissionRules.Pace`: as fast as its quote needs, then its own luck, less of it for a builder of standing), with its own crews, materials and plant. The builder books the inspector itself the hour each inspection falls due (`HiredBooks`); the verdict weighs its standing (`CommissionRules.Quality`); a failure is its rework at the stage's end, at its own cost, then booked again. A storm can still set it back. Its due day is the day its site's schedule hands the building over at the pace its quote needs (`CommissionRules.Due`), so a builder keeping its quote is never late and one slower than it is. The player hears of each stage begun, each booking, each verdict and payment, the day after its due day goes by (what it will give back a day, and the most), and the handover. A site an older version held at a stage's end for good (its hours lost counted in `WaitHours`, which a hired builder never runs up for want of materials) goes on from where it stands at its next working hour, told once; its due day stays as signed, every inspection and payment still comes once.
5. **The handover** is the company's own (`Handover`): the retention settled, the builder's record (built, its standing up on time and down late, the stock market's builder news outside a Going Public session), the critic's opinion and the building of the year with the builder named, the opening day and the ribbon, a plot's building handed to the district (the player's from the next load, used, let or sold, or its flats sold one by one), a rebuild's new look and refit. None of it is the company's: not its record, reputation, insurance or a fault's claim. A construction loan, an investor and flats sold off plan work as for the company's own builds.
6. **The app without a company:** the Overview says how to build without one and opens Land and Rivals; Land, Projects, the quotes and Rivals all work; Tenders and Crews say how to found the company. The Rivals tab counts what each builder has built for the player.

Going Public: the quotes are drawn from the save, the project and the week alone; the site is the player's own (a rebuild only of a building they own, under the programme's own-id rule; a guest starts no plot, as before); nothing is sent; the builder's news reaches the stock market only outside a session. A save with a contract read by an older RLO passes its new fields over; without the mod the line is kept as it was.

### Land too small for a site

A player (27 Oct 2026) had a shops project on a single space of Uptown, 7 by 35 m, through its plans, its permit and eight builders' quotes, and the hire then said only "The site could not be set out there. Player.log says why." The plot's frame needed 10 m of land (`PlotRules.NarrowMin`), and one space of the district's grid is 7 m (`SpacesRules.SpaceWidth`, the owner's "1 space is the standard lowest one"): no site on a single space could ever be set out, by a builder or by the company's crews. The developers' sites on such lots were passed over quietly (their building was still finished on its day).

- **The rule.** One answer, `PlotRules.Fit(width, depth, out need)` (`LandRules.SiteFit`): too narrow (under 7 m), too shallow (under 20 m deep for a narrow plot, 16 m for a wide one), too wide or too deep (over 60 m). The narrowest land is now one space, so every site the Land tab sells, one to seven spaces on a strip of 32, 35 or 39 m, takes a site (a metre kept each side, a 5 m frame on one space). What stays refused is land a site cannot stand on: a site bought short of its depth in build mode, or one over 60 m.
- **Where it is asked** (`ConstructionDirector.Fit.cs`): at the first step, planning a building on land (`NewPlot`), and at every step after it that draws, pays or starts, each before its fee: the use and the design, the plans, the engineer, City Hall's application, the appeal, the quotes and the hire (`HireBlock`, `HireBuilder`), the start (`StartBlock`). A refusal says the land's size against what a site needs and what would work (join the next space, buy more of its rows, split it), through the notice's own data (`AnswerData`); a builder's refusal names the builder. Nothing is charged for a refused step.
- **A project already stuck** (an older save, or land that changed): its page shows why in place of its plans, quotes and hire, with **Cancel and get the fees back** (`CancelUnfit`): the project is given up, the land stays the player's, and every fee paid for it (the practice's, the engineer's, City Hall's, an appeal's) comes back through the game's own money. A project something else has come in under (an investor, a flat sold off plan, a loan drawn) can only be given up as before. Only the player's own money moves, so it is the same in a Going Public session.
- **The city's builders.** Every list of designs for a site, the player's, the developers', a redevelopment's, a growth's and build mode's, is emptied first for land no site can be set out on (`KeepFitting`, `CornerDesigns`), so a developer's pick is none and the lot is passed over: the city never plans on land its site cannot use.
- **Texts.** Every reason a site was not set out is one of the programme's causes (`SiteRefusals`), each with plain words and "Nothing was charged"; none points at Player.log.

### Land market

A player held land at 8 Maple Street, the lot next to it was held by a rival developer so the player could not join them to build bigger, and the player could not sell land no longer wanted. Now land is sold back to the city, bought from a rival developer by offer, and the rivals trade land too. - **Sell this land** (the page of a site of the player's in the Land tab, with the price and a confirm step). Only while nothing is planned, permitted or under way on it (no project, car park or grown building), never in a Going Public session, and not for ground the district's plan stood a building on (it would not be for sale again). The city pays `LandMarketRules.CityPrice`: the higher of 85% of what the player paid and the land's market value now, each space at its own traffic and the rows of it held (`ValueOf`, the Land tab's own price for free spaces), nothing taken off. - **Offer to buy** (a rival's site is a row of the Land tab with its ask as a range, and the Next door section of the player's site beside it). Allowed while the rival has not started works there (planning is fine); never in a session. The player picks a price from seven steps (a tenth under the range to a tenth over it). The rival asks (`AskOf`) what it paid and a premium: 15% when its plans have just begun to 35% when City Hall is about to decide (`Progress`), 5 points more when it is the only site it holds, 6 less when it is short of cash (the land it holds at what it paid is more than its means, from its reputation and what it has built and won: `Means`), never under 10%. The range shown holds the ask but not in its middle (drawn from the save and the site).
