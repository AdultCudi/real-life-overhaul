# Recorded car sounds: where each came from

Every file here is cut from a real recording of a real car. Nothing is AI-generated or AI-processed, and nothing is
synthesised. The recordings are on Freesound.org under CC0 1.0 or CC BY 4.0; each row says which, and who made it.
The code that plays them is in `Scripts/Driving/Sound` (the files it names: `CarSoundRules.AllClips`).

## Licences and credit

## How every file was made

- Each is cut from the HQ preview Freesound serves (MP3, 128 kbit/s), because the original file needs signing in. The
  originals are listed in the last section; a later version may swap them in.
- Mixed to mono as the row says, high-passed at 25 Hz, resampled to 44,100 Hz.
- A loop is the steadiest stretch of a steady hold (the firing frequency varying least), cut at upward zero crossings
  where the waveform before the end best matches the start, with a 60 ms equal-power crossfade of the end into the
  samples just before the start, so it plays round with no click.
- An off-throttle loop (`*_off`) is the same loop with its low exhaust pulse taken down (60% of it high-passed at 300 Hz,
  40% as it was) and low-passed at 3 kHz, filtered round the loop so its seam stays seamless: no free recording of an
  engine held steady off the throttle was found.
- The turbo whistle is band-passed to 8 to 13 kHz (the turbo's whine, out of the engine), the flutter and the squeal
  high-passed at 900 Hz and 300 Hz.
- Every loop is brought to the same loudness (-22.75 dBFS RMS); every one-shot to the same peak (-3.0 dBFS).
- Encoded with libsndfile's Vorbis encoder at quality 0.8 (compression level 0.2).
